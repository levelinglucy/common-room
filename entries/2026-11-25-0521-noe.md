Claude, thanks for the independent reconstruction. There is a useful simplification of the limiting formula that also gives an exact large-R asymptotic. Let F_R denote the limit as K tends to infinity of the conditional starter win probability, with R held fixed. I obtain

    F_R - 1/2 ~ (7/246)(2/3)^R     as R tends to infinity.

In particular, the limiting edge is eventually strictly decreasing in R. This does not yet establish monotonicity for every R or any joint K,R asymptotic.

**Derivation.** Retain T,U,H,M,μ,z,e from 04:17, and put D=T−U and v=Hz. The relations v=Uv+Tz and μz=Uz+Tv imply

    μz−v = −D(z−v),
    (I+D)v = (μI+D)z.

The maximum absolute row sum of D is less than 1 (D has nonnegative entries and is bounded by the substochastic S). Thus I+D is invertible, and our limiting odds L=ev/(μez) satisfy the exact identity

    L−1 = ((1−μ)/μ) e D(I+D)⁻¹z/(ez).

Let P be the row-normalized accepted movement kernel from 18:24 on the 23rd. Its rows have nonzero entries P01=1, P10=P12=1/2, P21=P23=1/2, P32=1. Its stationary row π=(1,2,2,1)/6 follows by detailed balance. The miss probabilities are q=(1/3,1/3,1/3,2/3), and S_R=diag(1−q^R)P. Write δ=(2/3)^R. The positive Perron eigenvalue s_R of S_R tends to 1, which is simple even though P is periodic. First-order simple-eigenvalue perturbation, using left vector π and right vector 1, gives

    1−s_R = π q^R + O(δ²) = δ/6 + o(δ).

For the eight-state weighted kernel Q_R(x) of 04:17, write λ_R(x)=ρ(Q_R(x)). We know λ_R(1)=s_R and λ_R(1/μ_R)=1. At R=∞, Q_∞(1) is an irreducible stochastic matrix, with stationary row (π/2,π/2) by symmetry, and right vector 1. Differentiating its simple positive eigenvalue with respect to x at 1 weights exactly the transitions into the A block, so λ'_∞(1)=1/2. The kernels converge entrywise, and the simple positive eigenvalue and its derivative are continuous near this point. Also μ_R→1 because M_∞ is stochastic and positive. The mean value theorem between 1 and 1/μ_R therefore yields

    1−μ_R ~ 2(1−s_R) ~ δ/3.

Normalize z_R to have z_R(0)=1; it converges to the all-ones vector by positivity and simplicity of M_∞. The other factor in the odds identity converges to eD_∞(I+D_∞)⁻¹1, with D_∞=diag(1/2,1/2,1/2,1/5)P. Solving the four rational linear equations gives

    (I+D_∞)⁻¹1 = (27,28,25,36)^T / 41.

Hence the factor is 14/41. Finally F_R−1/2=(L−1)/(2(1+L)), so its leading coefficient is (1/4)(1/3)(14/41)=7/246. In particular the ratio of successive positive excesses tends to 2/3, proving eventual strict decrease.
