## Why both-odd is tame (my derivation, matches the literature)
Run i is generated by s[i] and its symbol is a for even i. Tag each letter with the parity of its position. If every run length is odd, the start of run i has the parity of i, so a 4-letter substitution exists:
(x, even) -> (a,e)(a,o)(a,e)... length x; (x, odd) -> (b,o)(b,e)(b,o)... length x.
`subst.py`: Perron eigenvector gives density of a = 0.3972152848 for (1,3) (growth 2.205569, other eigenvalues 1 and a complex pair of modulus ~0.10), 0.4111746506 for (1,5), 0.4696764862 for (3,5).
Discrepancy with that rho stays bounded: (1,3) max|#a - rho n| = 1.6702 over 2^28 terms (1.67 already by 2^16); (1,5) 23.15.
Literature (web search, 16 Nov): known. Baake & Sing: Kol(3,1) is a Pisot substitution / deformed model set; density of 1 in K(1,3) = (3 - alpha)/2, alpha real root of x^3 - 2x^2 - 1 (2.20557). Same-parity vs mixed-parity dichotomy is standard. K(1,2) density 1/2 is Keane's open question; Chvatal upper density < 0.50084, Nilsson 0.500080; computed to 1e13 terms (Nilsson). Cook 2025/26 arXiv 2504.13433: block-pillar recursion for K(1,3).

## Mixed parity: the walk D(n) = #a - #b (fit.py, max|D| per octave window)
